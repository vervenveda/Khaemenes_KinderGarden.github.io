Teacher & Family Learning Notes

Week 31 · Five distinct learning experiences

This week develops Insects, habitats, gardens, observation tools, and stewardship. The essential question is “What tiny living things can we observe?” Children may respond through speech, pointing, AAC, movement, drawing, building, signing, selecting, or adult dictation. Support is documented separately from mastery.

Across the week

Literacy: Observation words and detail labels

Mathematics: Count legs/wings and graph garden life

Inquiry: Animal structures and habitats

SEL: Gentle observation

Maker / expression: Bug field guide

Monday · Wonder Launch

Purpose: Launch the week by noticing what children already know about Insects, habitats, gardens, observation tools, and stewardship, naming the essential question, and building shared vocabulary through story, movement, objects, and conversation.

Guided practice: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Teacher-led picture/object sort plus a short manipulative demonstration connecting Observation words and detail labels and Count legs/wings and graph garden life.

Independent choice: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Children choose one center to draw, build, sort, match, act, or tell what they noticed.

Reflection: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Child completes: “I noticed ___.” Response may be spoken, signed, pointed, drawn, dictated, or selected.

Evidence note: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Observe entry vocabulary, safe participation, one literacy behavior, and one mathematical/inquiry behavior. Record evidence without turning participation into mastery.

Tuesday · Guided Skill Lab

Purpose: Practice the week’s literacy and mathematics skills for Insects, habitats, gardens, observation tools, and stewardship with explicit modeling, manipulatives, oral rehearsal, and small-group feedback.

Guided practice: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Small-group literacy/math lab with teacher prompts, manipulatives, picture cards, and repeated practice.

Independent choice: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: A choice board offers one literacy task, one math task, and one combined play task.

Reflection: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Child shows or tells one strategy that worked and one part they want to practice again.

Evidence note: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Collect a brief skill snapshot from the guided lab; mark support level and strategy used, not just correct/incorrect.

Wednesday · Inquiry Workshop

Purpose: Investigate Animal structures and habitats by observing, predicting, testing, sorting, mapping, comparing, or asking questions while using the week’s language and math.

Guided practice: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Teacher-supported inquiry station using safe materials, a prediction, an observation, and a simple evidence record.

Independent choice: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Children repeat or extend the investigation at a center, then place one observation in the class evidence display.

Reflection: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Child completes: “I thought ___. I noticed ___. Now I think ___.” with any accessible communication mode.

Evidence note: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Look for safe inquiry behavior, a real observation, and an evidence-based statement. Adult prompting is documented separately.

Thursday · Create & Communicate

Purpose: Create, build, move, draw, sing, dramatize, or present a product that communicates understanding of Insects, habitats, gardens, observation tools, and stewardship and connects to the maker project: Bug field guide.

Guided practice: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Teacher conference or maker mini-lesson focused on planning, material use, and communicating an idea clearly.

Independent choice: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Children work on Bug field guide using art, construction, movement, dramatic play, music, or mixed media.

Reflection: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Child identifies one choice they made and what they wanted another person to notice or understand.

Evidence note: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Document planning, persistence, communication, and connection to the week’s idea. Artistic neatness is not a mastery criterion.

Friday · Review, Assessment & Reflection

Purpose: Retrieve, apply, and reflect on the week’s learning about Insects, habitats, gardens, observation tools, and stewardship; complete formal evidence without confusing confidence, speed, speech volume, or compliance with mastery.

Guided practice: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Brief review stations target misconceptions found during the week before the formal demonstration.

Independent choice: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Child completes the week’s demonstration and selects a portfolio artifact or reflection response.

Reflection: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Child answers one growth prompt: “I can…,” “I learned…,” or “I want to practice…”

Evidence note: Week 31 — Bugs, Gardens, and Tiny Worlds: Use the published weekly rubric. Formal progression requires at least 80% plus any stated essential criteria; participation evidence alone never awards mastery.

Mastery safeguard

Daily participation and work samples show the learning journey. They do not automatically award mastery. Formal advancement continues to require the published weekly rubric at 80% or higher, including any essential criteria named for the week.
