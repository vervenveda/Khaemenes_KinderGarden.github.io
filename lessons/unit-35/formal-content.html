Teacher & Family Learning Notes

Week 35 · Five distinct learning experiences

This week develops Inquiry project: ask, plan, make, test, share, and revise. The essential question is “What big question do I want to explore?” Children may respond through speech, pointing, AAC, movement, drawing, building, signing, selecting, or adult dictation. Support is documented separately from mastery.

Across the week

Literacy: Question words and oral presentation

Mathematics: Choose math tools for a project

Inquiry: Inquiry, evidence, design, and communication

SEL: Confidence and collaboration

Maker / expression: Big question board

Monday · Wonder Launch

Purpose: Launch the week by noticing what children already know about Inquiry project: ask, plan, make, test, share, and revise, naming the essential question, and building shared vocabulary through story, movement, objects, and conversation.

Guided practice: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Teacher-led picture/object sort plus a short manipulative demonstration connecting Question words and oral presentation and Choose math tools for a project.

Independent choice: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Children choose one center to draw, build, sort, match, act, or tell what they noticed.

Reflection: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Child completes: “I noticed ___.” Response may be spoken, signed, pointed, drawn, dictated, or selected.

Evidence note: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Observe entry vocabulary, safe participation, one literacy behavior, and one mathematical/inquiry behavior. Record evidence without turning participation into mastery.

Tuesday · Guided Skill Lab

Purpose: Practice the week’s literacy and mathematics skills for Inquiry project: ask, plan, make, test, share, and revise with explicit modeling, manipulatives, oral rehearsal, and small-group feedback.

Guided practice: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Small-group literacy/math lab with teacher prompts, manipulatives, picture cards, and repeated practice.

Independent choice: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: A choice board offers one literacy task, one math task, and one combined play task.

Reflection: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Child shows or tells one strategy that worked and one part they want to practice again.

Evidence note: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Collect a brief skill snapshot from the guided lab; mark support level and strategy used, not just correct/incorrect.

Wednesday · Inquiry Workshop

Purpose: Investigate Inquiry, evidence, design, and communication by observing, predicting, testing, sorting, mapping, comparing, or asking questions while using the week’s language and math.

Guided practice: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Teacher-supported inquiry station using safe materials, a prediction, an observation, and a simple evidence record.

Independent choice: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Children repeat or extend the investigation at a center, then place one observation in the class evidence display.

Reflection: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Child completes: “I thought ___. I noticed ___. Now I think ___.” with any accessible communication mode.

Evidence note: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Look for safe inquiry behavior, a real observation, and an evidence-based statement. Adult prompting is documented separately.

Thursday · Create & Communicate

Purpose: Create, build, move, draw, sing, dramatize, or present a product that communicates understanding of Inquiry project: ask, plan, make, test, share, and revise and connects to the maker project: Big question board.

Guided practice: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Teacher conference or maker mini-lesson focused on planning, material use, and communicating an idea clearly.

Independent choice: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Children work on Big question board using art, construction, movement, dramatic play, music, or mixed media.

Reflection: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Child identifies one choice they made and what they wanted another person to notice or understand.

Evidence note: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Document planning, persistence, communication, and connection to the week’s idea. Artistic neatness is not a mastery criterion.

Friday · Review, Assessment & Reflection

Purpose: Retrieve, apply, and reflect on the week’s learning about Inquiry project: ask, plan, make, test, share, and revise; complete formal evidence without confusing confidence, speed, speech volume, or compliance with mastery.

Guided practice: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Brief review stations target misconceptions found during the week before the formal demonstration.

Independent choice: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Child completes the week’s demonstration and selects a portfolio artifact or reflection response.

Reflection: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Child answers one growth prompt: “I can…,” “I learned…,” or “I want to practice…”

Evidence note: Week 35 — My Big Question Capstone: Use the published weekly rubric. Formal progression requires at least 80% plus any stated essential criteria; participation evidence alone never awards mastery.

Mastery safeguard

Daily participation and work samples show the learning journey. They do not automatically award mastery. Formal advancement continues to require the published weekly rubric at 80% or higher, including any essential criteria named for the week.
