Teacher & Family Learning Notes

Week 28 · Five distinct learning experiences

This week develops Nutrition, hygiene, rest, safety, body awareness, and health choices. The essential question is “How do we care for our bodies?” Children may respond through speech, pointing, AAC, movement, drawing, building, signing, selecting, or adult dictation. Support is documented separately from mastery.

Across the week

Literacy: Health vocabulary

Mathematics: Sort food groups/colors and count healthy choices

Inquiry: Body needs and healthy habits

SEL: Self-care

Maker / expression: Healthy plate collage

Monday · Wonder Launch

Purpose: Launch the week by noticing what children already know about Nutrition, hygiene, rest, safety, body awareness, and health choices, naming the essential question, and building shared vocabulary through story, movement, objects, and conversation.

Guided practice: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Teacher-led picture/object sort plus a short manipulative demonstration connecting Health vocabulary and Sort food groups/colors and count healthy choices.

Independent choice: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Children choose one center to draw, build, sort, match, act, or tell what they noticed.

Reflection: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Child completes: “I noticed ___.” Response may be spoken, signed, pointed, drawn, dictated, or selected.

Evidence note: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Observe entry vocabulary, safe participation, one literacy behavior, and one mathematical/inquiry behavior. Record evidence without turning participation into mastery.

Tuesday · Guided Skill Lab

Purpose: Practice the week’s literacy and mathematics skills for Nutrition, hygiene, rest, safety, body awareness, and health choices with explicit modeling, manipulatives, oral rehearsal, and small-group feedback.

Guided practice: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Small-group literacy/math lab with teacher prompts, manipulatives, picture cards, and repeated practice.

Independent choice: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: A choice board offers one literacy task, one math task, and one combined play task.

Reflection: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Child shows or tells one strategy that worked and one part they want to practice again.

Evidence note: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Collect a brief skill snapshot from the guided lab; mark support level and strategy used, not just correct/incorrect.

Wednesday · Inquiry Workshop

Purpose: Investigate Body needs and healthy habits by observing, predicting, testing, sorting, mapping, comparing, or asking questions while using the week’s language and math.

Guided practice: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Teacher-supported inquiry station using safe materials, a prediction, an observation, and a simple evidence record.

Independent choice: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Children repeat or extend the investigation at a center, then place one observation in the class evidence display.

Reflection: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Child completes: “I thought ___. I noticed ___. Now I think ___.” with any accessible communication mode.

Evidence note: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Look for safe inquiry behavior, a real observation, and an evidence-based statement. Adult prompting is documented separately.

Thursday · Create & Communicate

Purpose: Create, build, move, draw, sing, dramatize, or present a product that communicates understanding of Nutrition, hygiene, rest, safety, body awareness, and health choices and connects to the maker project: Healthy plate collage.

Guided practice: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Teacher conference or maker mini-lesson focused on planning, material use, and communicating an idea clearly.

Independent choice: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Children work on Healthy plate collage using art, construction, movement, dramatic play, music, or mixed media.

Reflection: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Child identifies one choice they made and what they wanted another person to notice or understand.

Evidence note: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Document planning, persistence, communication, and connection to the week’s idea. Artistic neatness is not a mastery criterion.

Friday · Review, Assessment & Reflection

Purpose: Retrieve, apply, and reflect on the week’s learning about Nutrition, hygiene, rest, safety, body awareness, and health choices; complete formal evidence without confusing confidence, speed, speech volume, or compliance with mastery.

Guided practice: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Brief review stations target misconceptions found during the week before the formal demonstration.

Independent choice: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Child completes the week’s demonstration and selects a portfolio artifact or reflection response.

Reflection: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Child answers one growth prompt: “I can…,” “I learned…,” or “I want to practice…”

Evidence note: Week 28 — Health, Food, and Growing Bodies: Use the published weekly rubric. Formal progression requires at least 80% plus any stated essential criteria; participation evidence alone never awards mastery.

Mastery safeguard

Daily participation and work samples show the learning journey. They do not automatically award mastery. Formal advancement continues to require the published weekly rubric at 80% or higher, including any essential criteria named for the week.
