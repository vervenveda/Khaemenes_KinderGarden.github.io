Teacher & Family Learning Notes

Week 26 · Five distinct learning experiences

This week develops Classroom symbols, community symbols, respectful participation, and voice. The essential question is “How do symbols help people remember and belong?” Children may respond through speech, pointing, AAC, movement, drawing, building, signing, selecting, or adult dictation. Support is documented separately from mastery.

Across the week

Literacy: Symbol labels and shared pledge of care

Mathematics: Sort symbols by attributes

Inquiry: Civic symbols and classroom agreements

SEL: Respectful participation

Maker / expression: Classroom flag

Monday · Wonder Launch

Purpose: Launch the week by noticing what children already know about Classroom symbols, community symbols, respectful participation, and voice, naming the essential question, and building shared vocabulary through story, movement, objects, and conversation.

Guided practice: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Teacher-led picture/object sort plus a short manipulative demonstration connecting Symbol labels and shared pledge of care and Sort symbols by attributes.

Independent choice: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Children choose one center to draw, build, sort, match, act, or tell what they noticed.

Reflection: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Child completes: “I noticed ___.” Response may be spoken, signed, pointed, drawn, dictated, or selected.

Evidence note: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Observe entry vocabulary, safe participation, one literacy behavior, and one mathematical/inquiry behavior. Record evidence without turning participation into mastery.

Tuesday · Guided Skill Lab

Purpose: Practice the week’s literacy and mathematics skills for Classroom symbols, community symbols, respectful participation, and voice with explicit modeling, manipulatives, oral rehearsal, and small-group feedback.

Guided practice: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Small-group literacy/math lab with teacher prompts, manipulatives, picture cards, and repeated practice.

Independent choice: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: A choice board offers one literacy task, one math task, and one combined play task.

Reflection: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Child shows or tells one strategy that worked and one part they want to practice again.

Evidence note: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Collect a brief skill snapshot from the guided lab; mark support level and strategy used, not just correct/incorrect.

Wednesday · Inquiry Workshop

Purpose: Investigate Civic symbols and classroom agreements by observing, predicting, testing, sorting, mapping, comparing, or asking questions while using the week’s language and math.

Guided practice: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Teacher-supported inquiry station using safe materials, a prediction, an observation, and a simple evidence record.

Independent choice: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Children repeat or extend the investigation at a center, then place one observation in the class evidence display.

Reflection: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Child completes: “I thought ___. I noticed ___. Now I think ___.” with any accessible communication mode.

Evidence note: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Look for safe inquiry behavior, a real observation, and an evidence-based statement. Adult prompting is documented separately.

Thursday · Create & Communicate

Purpose: Create, build, move, draw, sing, dramatize, or present a product that communicates understanding of Classroom symbols, community symbols, respectful participation, and voice and connects to the maker project: Classroom flag.

Guided practice: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Teacher conference or maker mini-lesson focused on planning, material use, and communicating an idea clearly.

Independent choice: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Children work on Classroom flag using art, construction, movement, dramatic play, music, or mixed media.

Reflection: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Child identifies one choice they made and what they wanted another person to notice or understand.

Evidence note: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Document planning, persistence, communication, and connection to the week’s idea. Artistic neatness is not a mastery criterion.

Friday · Review, Assessment & Reflection

Purpose: Retrieve, apply, and reflect on the week’s learning about Classroom symbols, community symbols, respectful participation, and voice; complete formal evidence without confusing confidence, speed, speech volume, or compliance with mastery.

Guided practice: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Brief review stations target misconceptions found during the week before the formal demonstration.

Independent choice: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Child completes the week’s demonstration and selects a portfolio artifact or reflection response.

Reflection: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Child answers one growth prompt: “I can…,” “I learned…,” or “I want to practice…”

Evidence note: Week 26 — Symbols, Flags, and Civic Belonging: Use the published weekly rubric. Formal progression requires at least 80% plus any stated essential criteria; participation evidence alone never awards mastery.

Mastery safeguard

Daily participation and work samples show the learning journey. They do not automatically award mastery. Formal advancement continues to require the published weekly rubric at 80% or higher, including any essential criteria named for the week.
