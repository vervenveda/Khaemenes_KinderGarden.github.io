# Kindergarten Social Studies · National Portability Crosswalk

This crosswalk documents the broad frameworks used to design **My World, My Community**. It is not a claim that one national body sets a single mandatory Kindergarten Social Studies curriculum.

## NCSS C3 Inquiry Arc
The course repeatedly develops:
1. **Developing Questions and Planning Inquiries** — weekly essential questions and learner-generated questions.
2. **Applying Disciplinary Concepts and Tools** — civics, economics, geography, and history.
3. **Evaluating Sources and Using Evidence** — Wednesday source/evidence labs, notice/infer routines, maps, artifacts, photographs, timelines, oral histories, and simple data.
4. **Communicating Conclusions and Taking Informed Action** — Thursday civic/choice studio and Unit 8 evidence-to-action project.

Official framework: https://www.socialstudies.org/standards/c3

## Recurring Kindergarten content coverage
The 36-week scope intentionally covers content that appears repeatedly across state Kindergarten frameworks:
- self, others, family, and community;
- rules, fairness, authority, leadership, and group decision processes;
- maps, globes/models, symbols, direction, place, land/water, and human features;
- past/present/future, sequence, timelines, primary-source awareness, artifacts, photographs, and oral history;
- needs/wants, goods/services, jobs, producers/consumers, money, spending/saving, exchange, choice, and tradeoffs;
- culture, traditions, celebrations, language, stories, art, music, perspective, respectful comparison, and anti-stereotype habits;
- community symbols, public services, local government, representatives, accountability, and problem solving;
- environmental choices, stewardship, service, and safe informed action.

Reference frameworks:
- Florida Social Studies: https://www.fldoe.org/academics/standards/subject-areas/social-studies/
- New York Social Studies: https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/social-studies
- California History-Social Science: https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/hs/

## Assessment design
Kindergarten mastery is demonstrated through a combination of teacher observation, learner explanation/drawing, source or map work, portfolio artifacts, and randomized weekly knowledge checks. Read-aloud assistance is allowed. The 80% gate is a course progression rule; it is not used to punish emerging readers.

## Privacy and inclusion
Family and cultural learning never requires a learner to disclose private family information, beliefs, immigration status, address, or other sensitive details. Fictional examples may always be used.

## State/local localization

State localization is required. Kindergarten retains a broad national disciplinary core while adapting developmentally appropriate maps, symbols, public institutions, local history sources, Indigenous histories, environmental examples, and community evidence to the learner's state/locality.

See `STATE_LOCALIZATION_GUIDE.md`.

## State/localization requirement
Kindergarten requires an age-appropriate state/local overlay. Learners should encounter their state/community through maps, public helpers/services, symbols, local places, simple then-and-now evidence, environmental choices, and a safe community action or capstone example. The national Kindergarten strands remain intact and the 80% progression rule does not change.
