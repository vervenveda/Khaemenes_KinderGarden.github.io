# Kindergarten Social Studies · State & Local Localization Guide

## Academy rule

Kindergarten Social Studies is nationally portable and **state-localizable by requirement**. Keep the common course intact, then make the learner's state and community visible through developmentally appropriate maps, pictures, public places, helpers, symbols, stories, historical clues, and civic examples.

Localization must remain playful and age-appropriate. It may not lower the 80% mastery threshold or turn the course into memorization of state trivia.

## Required localization checkpoints

- **Weeks 3–4 · School/community helpers:** include familiar local public/community roles or services.
- **Weeks 9–12 · Geography/place:** locate the learner's state on a U.S. map; use local/state land, water, human features, directions, and map examples.
- **Weeks 15–16 · Sources/then-and-now:** use a public local photograph, landmark, map, object, museum image, or teacher-created fictional equivalent.
- **Weeks 25–28 · Symbols, government, leadership, problem solving:** add age-appropriate state/local symbols, public services, leaders/representatives, and a simple shared local problem.
- **Weeks 30–32 · Environment/service/action:** use a state/local place or shared-space example.
- **Week 35 · Capstone:** allow the learner to teach something accurate about their community or state as part of the broader course synthesis.

## Indigenous history safeguard

If state/local examples include Indigenous peoples, use the specific nation/community name when known and make clear that Indigenous peoples and nations continue today. Avoid costumes, generic "tribe" activities, or treating culture as decoration.

## Privacy

Learners never have to share a home address, immigration/citizenship status, religion, political affiliation, finances, or private family history. Public, fictional, or teacher-curated examples are always acceptable.
