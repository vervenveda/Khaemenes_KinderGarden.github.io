# Kindergarten Social Studies · State & Local Localization Guide

State/local localization is a **required design feature** of Kindergarten Social Studies. The national core in community, rules, geography, historical time, economics, culture, public life, inquiry, and informed action remains canonical; educators use the learner's state and locality to make those ideas concrete.

## Priority localization points
- **Weeks 3–4:** school/community helpers, local public services, and shared community needs.
- **Weeks 9–12:** maps of the school/community/state; local land and water; state location; nearby human and natural features.
- **Weeks 13–16:** then-and-now community photographs, public artifacts, local landmarks, and age-appropriate historical sources.
- **Weeks 25–28:** state/local symbols, community services, local government, representatives, and public problem solving.
- **Weeks 30–32:** local environments, parks/public spaces, stewardship, service, and safe informed action.
- **Week 35:** capstone may teach how the learner's local community/state works using public evidence.

## What may be localized
Educators may add or substitute:
- state flag/seal and age-appropriate state symbols;
- state capital and selected local/state landmarks;
- local maps, waterways, coasts, landforms, parks, and public spaces;
- firefighters, librarians, sanitation/public works, parks staff, transit, school district, municipal/county/state services;
- specific Indigenous nation(s)/homelands connected to the region, presented as living communities where appropriate;
- local historical photographs, museum objects, oral-history excerpts, and public stories;
- local economic examples such as farms, ports, stores, services, tourism, or manufacturing when developmentally appropriate.

## Florida example
Florida Kindergarten implementations may use the Florida peninsula, Atlantic Ocean and Gulf coast, local wetlands/waterways, state symbols, Tallahassee as the state capital, municipal/county/state helpers, Florida parks and conservation examples, and specific Florida Indigenous histories/nations where relevant.

## Safeguards
Do not require home addresses, immigration/citizenship status, religion, political affiliation, family finances, or private family history. Young learners may always use fictional examples. Keep civic/election learning nonpartisan. Avoid stereotypes and avoid presenting Indigenous peoples only as people of the past.

## Non-negotiables
Localization cannot remove the Kindergarten national disciplinary core, replace inquiry with trivia, or lower the 80% weekly mastery threshold.
