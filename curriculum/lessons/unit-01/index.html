Unit 01

Welcome to the Learning Garden

Belonging, routines, names, classroom materials, and learning how to learn together

Essential Question

Who am I in this learning community, and how can I help make it a place where everyone can learn?

KHAE-DAPKHAE-ELOFKHAE-LIT-KKHAE-MATH-KKHAE-SEL-KKHAE-ART-KKHAE-HEALTH-KKHAE-CIV-K

Literacy: Recognize and use names; connect spoken language to print; listen and respond

Math: Count objects with one-to-one correspondence; match and sort by observable attributes

Inquiry: Observe how the classroom works; identify routines, needs, responsibilities, and safe choices

SEL: Identify feelings and needs; practice asking for help and offering help

Maker Project: Create a personal name/identity artifact and collaborate on a simple classroom community map

Monday: Discover — Who Belongs Here?

Objective: Children identify themselves as members of a learning community and begin using names, classroom language, and routines to communicate belonging.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and the question: “What helps people feel welcome and ready to learn?”

Literacy: Find and recognize their own name; notice that spoken names can be represented with print.

Math: Count a small collection of classroom objects and match one object to one counting word.

Inquiry: Walk through the classroom and notice places, materials, people, and routines.

SEL: Name a feeling or need and practice a help-seeking sentence.

Workshop: Build a shared “Learning Garden” anchor chart from children's observations. Children create a first name/self-portrait artifact.

Centers: Name/print table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot.

Teacher evidence: Can the child identify their name, communicate a need, count a small collection, and describe one classroom feature?

Tuesday: Investigate — How Do We Know Where Things Belong?

Objective: Children investigate how classroom materials are organized and use attributes to sort and classify objects.

Morning Circle: Revisit Monday observations; introduce “same,” “different,” “belongs,” and “does not belong.”

Literacy: Match name cards to children and classroom labels; listen for beginning sounds in familiar names.

Math: Sort classroom objects by one attribute, then explain the sorting rule.

Inquiry: Investigate why materials have designated places and how organization helps a community work.

SEL: Practice asking before taking a shared material and returning materials safely.

Workshop: Small groups sort a mixed collection of classroom materials, photograph/draw the groups, and explain the rule they used.

Centers: Label-and-match station, sorting lab, construction/maker station, dramatic play, movement/music, book corner, calm reflection spot.

Teacher evidence: Can the child sort by an observable attribute and explain the rule using words, gesture, drawing, or demonstration?

Wednesday: Reason — What Makes a Classroom Work?

Objective: Children connect routines and responsibilities to classroom safety, learning, and belonging.

Morning Circle: Examine two simple classroom scenarios: one that supports learning and one that creates a problem. Ask, “What happened? What could we change?”

Literacy: Sequence picture cards showing a classroom routine and retell the steps.

Math: Compare two groups of classroom objects using more, fewer, or same.

Inquiry: Identify cause-and-effect relationships between actions, routines, and classroom outcomes.

SEL: Practice cooperation, turn-taking, and a calm strategy for solving a small classroom problem.

Workshop: Children work in pairs to sequence a routine, then explain why the routine matters.

Centers: Routine sequencing cards, math comparison station, building challenge, dramatic play, movement/music, book corner, calm reflection spot.

Teacher evidence: Can the child explain a routine as a sequence and give a reason why it helps people learn or stay safe?

Thursday: Apply — How Can We Help Our Learning Community?

Objective: Children apply what they have learned by creating a representation of the classroom and choosing a helpful responsibility.

Morning Circle: Review observations, sorting rules, and routines. Ask, “What can I do that helps someone else learn?”

Literacy: Add labels, dictated language, or attempted writing to a classroom map or responsibility card.

Math: Count and place a small number of map features; compare quantities when deciding where materials belong.

Inquiry: Collaboratively create a simple classroom map using symbols for important spaces.

SEL: Choose and rehearse one realistic classroom responsibility.

Workshop: Build the class map in teams. Children negotiate where features belong and explain their choices.

Centers: Map-making, name/label station, math materials, dramatic play, movement/music, book corner, calm reflection spot.

Teacher evidence: Can the child contribute an idea, use a simple representation, and explain how their action helps the community?

Friday: Demonstrate — I Am Part of the Learning Garden

Objective: Children demonstrate what they understand about belonging, routines, organization, counting, and helping others.

Morning Circle: Children revisit the week's question and share one thing they noticed, learned, or can now do.

Literacy: Identify their name and respond to a short read-aloud question about community or belonging.

Math: Complete a hands-on count, match, sort, or comparison task and show thinking with objects, words, drawing, or gesture.

Inquiry: Use the classroom map or routine sequence to explain how one part of the learning community works.

SEL: Demonstrate a help-seeking or helping behavior.

Workshop: Complete the weekly mastery check, add one artifact to the portfolio, and celebrate a specific growth moment.

Centers: Choice-based review centers with teacher conferencing and targeted observation.

Teacher evidence: Collect one literacy sample, one math demonstration, one inquiry explanation, and one SEL/participation observation.
