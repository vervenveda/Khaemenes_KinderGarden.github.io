Monday: Wonder Launch

Daily learning target: Choose two portfolio samples and describe evidence of personal growth.

Recall & vocabulary: Recall a skill you practised earlier this year. Key words: sample, portfolio, growth, evidence. Compare your own work; do not compare children.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How can I show I am ready for first grade?”

Literacy: Review letters, sounds, words, and stories

Math: Review numbers, shapes, data, and problem solving

Inquiry: Review science/social studies/arts/wellness

SEL: Pride, gratitude, and next steps

Original activity: Compare one early and one recent learning sample without ranking children against peers.

Practice check (not formal mastery): Selects examples and expresses a specific new skill.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Teach · Practice · Show independently

Adult modeling: Show an early drawing with one unlabeled shape and a recent drawing that labels two shapes. Say: “This newer work shows I can name shapes now.” Growth means comparing personal evidence, not another child's work.

Guided practice (three varied examples):

  1. Compare two original example writing samples, identifying a newly added label.
  2. Compare an early three-counter count and a newer accurate five-counter count; name the improved skill.
  3. Use an evidence sentence frame: “Before I ___; now I can ___ because this shows ___.”

Fresh independent transfer: Choose two actual personal work samples without adult selection; communicate one specific growth claim supported by a visible or demonstrated difference. If no older sample exists, compare an initial attempt and a fresh attempt without manufacturing evidence.

Corrective feedback: If the learner says only “I am better,” invite one specific feature visible in the work; do not imply first-grade readiness or award certification from this reflection.

Accessible evidence: Document the learner's own words, drawing, pointing, demonstration, or AAC response, with suitable accommodations. Daily practice and celebration are not formal mastery, final readiness, or certificate approval.

Tuesday: Guided Skill Lab

Daily learning target: Demonstrate familiar letter–sound connections and retell a short story in sequence.

Recall & vocabulary: Recall a familiar letter sound and a story with a beginning, middle, and end. Key words: sound, sequence, retell, detail. Use only letter-sound connections already taught.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How can I show I am ready for first grade?”

Literacy: Review letters, sounds, words, and stories

Math: Review numbers, shapes, data, and problem solving

Inquiry: Review science/social studies/arts/wellness

SEL: Pride, gratitude, and next steps

Original activity: Use educator-written story cards, familiar graphemes, and beginning-middle-end picture sequencing.

Practice check (not formal mastery): Shows a taught sound correspondence and two ordered story events.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Teach · Practice · Show independently

Adult modeling: Show the grapheme m and say /m/ (not the letter name), then blend /m/ /ă/ /p/ into “map” if those sound correspondences were taught. Read this original three-sentence story aloud: “Mia planted a seed. She watered the soil. A green sprout appeared.” Model beginning, middle, end with three new picture cards.

Guided practice (three varied examples):

  1. From previously taught graphemes, match a familiar letter to its sound; review with oral modeling if needed.
  2. Read the original three-sentence story aloud again and ask what Mia did first.
  3. Ask what happened after watering and arrange beginning, middle and end cards in sequence.

Fresh independent transfer: Offer a different, original story: “Lee packed a kite. The wind blew. Lee flew the kite.” Read it aloud once, then ask the learner to sequence the three events using new picture cards; also demonstrate two previously taught letter–sound connections without copying the example.

Corrective feedback: If events are reversed, reread the source story and ask “What happened before the wind?”; for uncertain letter sounds, use an appropriate familiar grapheme instead of testing untaught sounds.

Accessible evidence: Document the learner's own words, drawing, pointing, demonstration, or AAC response, with suitable accommodations. Daily practice and celebration are not formal mastery, final readiness, or certificate approval.

Wednesday: Inquiry Workshop

Daily learning target: Solve a number problem with objects and explain a prior science observation.

Recall & vocabulary: Recall how to count objects without counting one twice. Key words: total, add, observe, prediction, feature.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How can I show I am ready for first grade?”

Literacy: Review letters, sounds, words, and stories

Math: Review numbers, shapes, data, and problem solving

Inquiry: Review science/social studies/arts/wellness

SEL: Pride, gratitude, and next steps

Original activity: Represent a sum using counters and revisit a real observation from the learning garden.

Practice check (not formal mastery): Models a number solution and identifies an observation.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Teach · Practice · Show independently

Adult modeling: Place four counters and add two more: count 4, 5, 6 while showing 4 + 2 = 6. Separately show an original drawing of a leaf and say, “I see a long center line” is observation; “It is old” is an inference without enough evidence.

Guided practice (three varied examples):

  1. Build a new addition story with three counters and two counters; count the combined set.
  2. Show a five-counter set and remove one; demonstrate a subtraction change with objects.
  3. Inspect an educator-drawn plant image; separate one directly visible detail from an unsupported guess.

Fresh independent transfer: Give an unfamiliar story with five blocks and three more blocks; independently model and explain the total using counters. Then inspect a new butterfly drawing and identify one feature directly visible in the illustration, distinguishing it from a prediction.

Corrective feedback: If a child counts the same object twice, move each counter aside after counting; if a guess is presented as fact, prompt for the visible clue. This is practice, not final-exam scoring.

Accessible evidence: Document the learner's own words, drawing, pointing, demonstration, or AAC response, with suitable accommodations. Daily practice and celebration are not formal mastery, final readiness, or certificate approval.

Thursday: Create & Communicate

Daily learning target: Assemble and revise a showcase connecting language, mathematics, and inquiry.

Recall & vocabulary: Recall one artifact that shows something you learned. Key words: showcase, revise, explain, evidence, representation.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How can I show I am ready for first grade?”

Literacy: Review letters, sounds, words, and stories

Math: Review numbers, shapes, data, and problem solving

Inquiry: Review science/social studies/arts/wellness

SEL: Pride, gratitude, and next steps

Original activity: Create a labeled drawing, a number model, and a nature or design artifact; improve one element.

Practice check (not formal mastery): Provides work in three domains with one revision.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Teach · Practice · Show independently

Adult modeling: Lay out a three-part example showcase: a labeled drawing of a tree, a counter model showing 2 + 3 = 5, and a nature observation “I see three leaves.” Explain that each artifact answers a different question and must be the child's own evidence.

Guided practice (three varied examples):

  1. Choose an original language artifact and check that at least one label communicates an idea.
  2. Check a separate number model by recounting its counters and matching the result to a numeral.
  3. Inspect an inquiry drawing and revise a statement from a guess to an observation.

Fresh independent transfer: Independently assemble three personal or freshly created artifacts: a labeled picture or dictated sentence, a correctly explained counter representation, and an observation-based inquiry sample. Choose and explain one correction or addition without copying the adult example.

Corrective feedback: If the learner lacks a saved sample, invite a fresh demonstration in that domain; never create fictional student evidence or require public sharing.

Accessible evidence: Document the learner's own words, drawing, pointing, demonstration, or AAC response, with suitable accommodations. Daily practice and celebration are not formal mastery, final readiness, or certificate approval.

Friday: Review, Assessment & Reflection

Daily learning target: Share a learning achievement and set one future goal without compulsory performance.

Recall & vocabulary: Recall one real learning achievement and one skill you want to keep practising. Key words: reflect, goal, communicate, next step. This celebration activity does not itself decide formal readiness.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How can I show I am ready for first grade?”

Literacy: Review letters, sounds, words, and stories

Math: Review numbers, shapes, data, and problem solving

Inquiry: Review science/social studies/arts/wellness

SEL: Pride, gratitude, and next steps

Original activity: Hold an optional private or group showcase; distinguish celebrating learning from formal final certification.

Practice check (not formal mastery): Communicates one achievement and one next learning goal.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Teach · Practice · Show independently

Adult modeling: Share a fictional student's portfolio statement: “I learned to compare groups using counters. I want to practice explaining how I know.” Demonstrate celebrating a genuine achievement without claiming the final assessment has been passed.

Guided practice (three varied examples):

  1. Use a portfolio artifact to identify one real skill demonstrated.
  2. Help formulate an achievable next goal, for example “I will practice retelling stories with a beginning, middle, and end.”
  3. Practice giving a private explanation or pointing to a portfolio page; affirm that a performance before peers is optional.

Fresh independent transfer: With no example statement in view, independently select a real portfolio artifact, explain one evidence-backed achievement, and set one specific next learning goal. The learner may communicate privately or through AAC, drawing, or dictation.

Corrective feedback: If the learner feels uncertain, invite an adult to notice effort and specific progress without announcing grades, readiness, mastery, or a certificate. Formal final readiness follows the separate assessment authority.

Accessible evidence: Document the learner's own words, drawing, pointing, demonstration, or AAC response, with suitable accommodations. Daily practice and celebration are not formal mastery, final readiness, or certificate approval.
