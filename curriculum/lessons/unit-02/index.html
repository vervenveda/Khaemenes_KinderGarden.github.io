Monday: Discover — My Name Has Letters

Objective: Children recognize that names are made from letters and that print carries meaning. Children identify some letters in their own names and begin noticing that letters can be arranged in a particular order.

Morning Circle: Display several children's names. Ask: “What do you notice?” Invite children to compare names by length, first letter, and familiar letters. Introduce the vocabulary letter, name, first, last, same, different.

Literacy: Identify their own name in a group of name cards. Notice the first letter of their name. Match uppercase letters to familiar letters in their name. Practice tracking print from left to right during a short shared reading.

Math: Count the letters in a name using one-to-one correspondence. Compare two names using more, fewer, or same.

Inquiry: Investigate classroom print. Children search for labels, signs, book titles, names, and other examples of print that communicate information.

SEL: Practice listening while a partner shares their name and one thing they like. Children respond with “I heard you say...” or another respectful listening response.

Student Practice: Name recognition page: find, trace, build, and attempt to write their own name. Optional extension: circle the first letter of their name among several letters.

Workshop: Children create the first page of their personal Name Book: name, self-portrait, and an adult-dictated or emergent-writing sentence.

Centers: Name/letter station, magnetic letters, counting names, writing table, dramatic play, book corner, art/maker station, movement/music, calm reflection spot.

Teacher evidence: Can the child locate their own name, identify at least one familiar letter, notice a feature of print, and communicate an idea about themselves?

Tuesday: Investigate — Letters Make Words

Objective: Children investigate how letters represent the spoken words we use and begin connecting familiar letters with their sounds.

Morning Circle: Revisit children's names from Monday. Choose several familiar letters and play “Letter Detective.” Children look for the target letter in names, classroom labels, and books.

Literacy: Introduce or review a small set of high-utility letters from children's names. Practice matching uppercase and lowercase forms and connecting familiar letters to their beginning sounds when developmentally appropriate.

Math: Build collections of 1–10 letter tiles or counters. Count each collection and match it to a numeral. Ask, “How do you know there are ___?”

Inquiry: Examine a picture book as an object. Identify the author, illustrator, cover, and title. Discuss how pictures and words work together to communicate.

SEL: Practice taking turns during a partner letter hunt. Children use “my turn,” “your turn,” and “I found...” language.

Student Practice: Beginning-sound activity using familiar picture vocabulary. Children say each picture name, listen for the beginning sound, and circle the matching letter.

Workshop: Small groups complete a classroom “Letter Hunt.” Children record letters they find using drawing, circling, stickers, tracing, or emergent writing.

Centers: Letter/sound lab, magnetic-letter building, math counting station, story retelling, writing table, dramatic play, book corner, movement/music.

Teacher evidence: Can the child recognize familiar letters and participate in a sound-matching activity using pictures and oral language?

Wednesday: Reason — Stories Have Parts

Objective: Children recognize that stories have characters, settings, events, and a sequence. Children use pictures and oral language to retell a familiar story.

Morning Circle: Read a high-quality picture book aloud. Pause for predictions and observations rather than testing children on recall. Ask: “Who is in the story? Where are they? What happened first?”

Literacy: Identify character, setting, beginning, middle, and end. Sequence three major events from the read-aloud using picture cards.

Math: Count three groups of story-related objects. Compare groups and represent quantities with objects, drawings, fingers, or numerals.

Inquiry: Compare the roles of an author and illustrator. Discuss how the author uses words and the illustrator uses pictures to help tell the story.

SEL: Discuss how a character might feel during an important event. Children support their ideas with evidence from the character's actions, words, or facial expression.

Student Practice: Story sequencing page. Children cut, arrange, match, or draw three story events. Oral retelling is encouraged before written response.

Workshop: Partner story retelling. One child uses picture cards while the other tells what happened. Partners switch roles.

Centers: Story sequencing, puppet theater, character drawing, math story problems, writing table, book corner, construction/maker station, calm reflection spot.

Teacher evidence: Can the child identify a character or setting and communicate a logical sequence of events?

Thursday: Apply — I Can Be an Author

Objective: Children apply their understanding of names, letters, pictures, and story structure to create an original page for their Name Book.

Morning Circle: Revisit the essential question: “How do letters and stories help us share who we are?” Model how an author chooses an idea, draws a picture, and adds words through writing, labels, or dictation.

Literacy: Children compose a simple personal sentence using a frame such as “I like ___.”, “I can ___.”, or “My favorite ___ is ___.” Children may write independently, copy a model, label a drawing, or dictate to an adult.

Math: Count and represent something from the child's story. Example: “I have 4 blocks.” Children build, count, draw, and record the quantity.

Inquiry: Discuss how authors make choices about what they want readers to know. Children identify the message they want their page to communicate.

SEL: Practice giving kind, specific feedback: “I noticed...” and “I wonder...”

Student Practice: Personal writing page with picture space, sentence frame, handwriting lines, and optional word/picture bank.

Workshop: Children create the second page of their Name Book. Teacher conferences individually and encourages children to add detail, labels, letters, or additional information.

Centers: Author's studio, alphabet/word-building station, math story station, book-making table, dramatic play, art/maker station, book corner.

Teacher evidence: Can the child communicate an intentional idea through drawing, dictation, emergent writing, copied text, or independent writing?

Friday: Demonstrate — My Story, My Voice

Objective: Children demonstrate growing understanding of letters, names, print, counting, and story structure by sharing part of their Name Book.

Morning Circle: Review the week's learning. Children revisit the essential question and share one new thing they learned about letters, books, stories, or themselves.

Literacy: Identify their name, recognize several familiar letters, participate in a beginning-sound task, and retell a familiar story using pictures or oral language.

Math: Complete a short hands-on counting task within 10. Children demonstrate one-to-one correspondence and explain how they know the quantity.

Inquiry: Children identify an author and illustrator and explain one way each contributes to a book.

SEL: Share a completed page with a partner. Practice looking at the speaker, listening, asking a question, and responding respectfully.

Student Practice: “My Story” reflection page: draw something important about themselves and communicate one sentence through writing, labeling, or dictation.

Workshop: Children assemble their Name Books and participate in a small Author Share. Each child shares one page or illustration with a partner or small group.

Centers: Choice-based literacy review, counting lab, story theater, writing studio, art/maker station, book corner, movement/music, calm reflection spot.

Teacher evidence: Collect one literacy sample, one mathematical demonstration, one story-retelling observation, one writing sample, and one collaboration observation for the portfolio.
