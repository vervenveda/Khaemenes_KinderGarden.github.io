Universal Materials & Safety

A live plant or seed is optional. Learners may use photos, teacher observations, a houseplant, outdoor plant viewed safely, paper models, or a provided growth sequence. No purchase or outdoor access is required.

Plant/seed safety: do not taste classroom seeds or unknown plant parts. Adults check allergy and choking risks, supervise small seeds/tools, avoid poisonous/irritating species, and use handwashing when soil or plant material is handled. Moldy specimens are discarded by an adult.
Access: tactile models, large pieces, high contrast, adult-assisted measuring, AAC, sign, eye gaze, dictation, and home-language explanation are valid.

Monday · Seed & Structure Detectives

Plants have structures that do jobs

Children notice that plants vary, while common structures can support anchoring, transport, light capture, reproduction, and seed protection.

Plant parts are not a checklist

Roots
anchor / take in water & minerals
Stem
support / transport
Leaves
capture light / exchange gases
Flower / cone
reproduction in many plants
Fruit / seed
protect or begin new growth

Not every plant has every structure visible at the same time, and plants do not all look alike.

Seed variation

Compare safe images or adult-selected seeds by size, shape, texture, or covering. Do not assume the largest seed makes the largest plant.

Vocabulary: seed, root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit, sprout, seedling.

Structure-function match

  1. Choose or point to two plant structures.
  2. Describe what each helps the plant do.
  3. Draw/model a plant that does not have to look like the teacher's example.
  4. Mark which statements are observations and which are explanations.

Scaffold

Use two choices at a time: “Which part usually takes in water—roots or flower?” Then move toward explanation.

Family/community connection

Notice one plant used locally for food, shade, medicine, fiber, beauty, habitat, ceremony, or another purpose. Do not require harvesting or identification of unknown plants.

Monday evidence checkpoint

Save after the learner identifies at least two plant structures and gives an age-appropriate function.

Tuesday · Plant Needs Lab

What helps a plant live and grow?

Children distinguish resources and suitable conditions from common misconceptions.

Needs and conditions

Most green plants need access to water, light, air, mineral nutrients, suitable temperature, and space. Different species need different amounts.

Accuracy note: soil can supply water, minerals, support, and habitat, but soil is not simply “plant food.” Green plants use light energy, water, and carbon dioxide from air to make sugars.

Seeds have a beginning supply

A seed contains an embryo and stored energy that can support early germination. Some seeds germinate under soil or in darkness before leaves need light for continued growth. Different seeds have different germination requirements.

Need, condition, or not necessary?

  1. Sort cards/examples: water, light for green growth, air, suitable temperature, space, mineral nutrients, music, candy, toy.
  2. Explain one choice.
  3. Compare two different plants/cacti/ferns/aquatic plants using safe images.
  4. State: “Different plants can need different amounts.”

Prediction

Ask: “What might happen if a plant has too little water?” Record the prediction separately from what has actually been observed.

No forced experiment

Understanding plant needs does not require intentionally damaging a plant. Photos, teacher demonstrations, previously collected data, or hypothetical comparisons are valid.

Tuesday evidence checkpoint

Save after the learner explains several plant needs/conditions and avoids the misconception that soil itself is food.

Wednesday · Growth Journal Lab

Measure change, not expectations

Children measure or compare a plant/model over time and learn that growth schedules vary.

Same baseline, same unit

Measure from the same starting point using the same paper strip, cube, counter, grid unit, ruler if appropriate, or adult-assisted accessible method. Record the date/order and measurement.

No five-day germination promise

Different seeds germinate and grow at different rates, and some seeds may not germinate at all. A learner is never penalized because a real plant does not change during the school week.

Observation journal

  1. Make Observation 1 with drawing, marks, dictation, label, measurement, or adult note.
  2. Make Observation 2 later—or compare two provided growth images if live change is unavailable.
  3. Use taller/shorter/same, more/fewer leaves, new/no visible change, or another evidence statement.
  4. Keep a prediction in a separate box.

Evidence habit: “No visible change” is valid data.

Sequence

Order a simplified cycle: seed → germination/sprout → seedling → mature plant → reproduction/new seeds, while noting that plant life cycles vary.

Accessible evidence

An adult may hold the measuring tool while the learner directs the baseline and endpoint. Photos are of the plant only when locally appropriate; child images are never required.

Wednesday evidence checkpoint

Save after the learner records two ordered observations or compares provided growth stages using a consistent measure.

Thursday · Fair Test & Care Lab

Change one thing, compare carefully

Children begin experimental reasoning without needing to stress or damage living plants.

Fair-test idea

If two pretend plants differ in water and light and temperature, it is hard to know which difference mattered. Changing one condition at a time makes comparison more informative.

Model investigation

Use cards, teacher data, or safe models: Plant A and Plant B are alike except one receives less water. Ask what can be compared and what would still need repeated observations.

Evidence table

  1. Name the condition being changed.
  2. Name what stays the same.
  3. Predict a possible result.
  4. Read or collect safe observation evidence.
  5. Decide whether the evidence supports, challenges, or is not enough to settle the prediction.

Science language: one small observation does not prove a rule for every plant.

Care and stewardship

Water only as appropriate for the chosen plant; do not overwater “to be kind.” Respect community gardens, farms, wild plants, and culturally significant plants. Ask permission before picking.

Safety

Unknown plants are look-don't-touch unless an informed adult approves. Classroom specimens are not food. Use nonliving models for allergy, sensory, immune, mobility, housing, or access needs.

Thursday evidence checkpoint

Save after the learner explains a one-condition-at-a-time comparison and demonstrates safe plant-care reasoning.

Friday · Plant Scientist Conference

Retrieve, measure, reason, revise

Friday integrates the week before formal mastery.

Five quick asks

  1. Name two plant structures and functions.
  2. Name several resources/conditions green plants need.
  3. Put growth stages in a reasonable order.
  4. Explain how to measure change fairly over time.
  5. Tell observation from prediction.

Misconception repair

Respond to: “Soil is the food a plant eats.” A stronger explanation is that soil may provide water, mineral nutrients, support, and habitat, while green plants make sugars using light, water, and carbon dioxide.

Plant scientist performance

  1. Use a real safe plant, image sequence, or model.
  2. Describe one structure and function.
  3. Report one observation and one separate prediction.
  4. Measure or compare growth with a consistent method.
  5. Explain one fair-test or stewardship choice.

Reflection

Complete: “I first thought ____. My evidence showed ____. Now I think ____.” Revision is scientific growth, not failure.

Portfolio

Save a plant journal page, safe model, measurement record, sequence card, or teacher note. A live plant, successful germination, home garden, or purchased kit is never required.

Friday evidence checkpoint

Save only after retrieval/remediation and the Plant Scientist performance. This confirms the learning sequence is complete; it does not award mastery.
