Retrieve first-semester learning without answer rehearsal, identify what is secure and what needs more practice, use targeted remediation, integrate ideas across domains, and prepare for the separate midyear demonstration.

Week 18 Learning Arc

Monday: take a low-stakes retrieval snapshot across first-semester domains.

Tuesday: target literacy/language needs with explicit remediation and transfer.

Wednesday: target mathematics/quantitative reasoning needs with concrete representations.

Thursday: integrate inquiry, community, design, evidence, safety, and model reasoning.

Friday: revisit weak spots, organize evidence, reflect on growth, and determine readiness for Week 18 mastery.

Continuity: Weeks 5–17 built daily evidence and week-specific mastery. Week 18 is practice, retrieval, and remediation—not the midyear demonstration. It strengthens transfer across previously learned skills before the separate milestone becomes eligible.

Teach · Practise · Correct · Transfer

Prerequisite retrieval: Retrieve skills without scoring first. Explain the purpose: identify what the child can already do and what teaching would help next.

Explicit teacher model: Model a low-stakes snapshot with an example, and show three labels: independent, prompted, and not yet observed. Make clear that the snapshot itself does not award mastery or label ability.

Guided-to-independent practice: Administer a small sample of literacy, number, and inquiry tasks without hints at first; note responses and access supports, then select only one priority skill to reteach.

Common misconception and correction: If speed or public speaking is mistaken for knowledge, offer quiet, untimed, pointing/AAC/manipulative response options. If one mistake is generalized to ability, collect another sample before interpreting.

Fresh transfer check: Give a new parallel item for one sampled skill after recording the initial response. Compare what changed and identify whether the scaffold or explanation helped.

Evidence rule: Record the target skills separately and distinguish independent responses from prompted ones. Fresh transfer is collected before hints.

Diagnostic evidence & next teaching decision

Create a diagnostic profile by skill, not one overall score. For each sampled literacy, math, and inquiry target, mark independent, prompted, or not yet observed. Do not count this snapshot as weekly mastery or midyear milestone evidence.

Response status: Record each target as independent, prompted, or not yet observed. “Not yet observed” means more evidence or teaching is needed; it is not a label about the child.

Scoring boundary: This snapshot is instructional planning evidence only—not mastery credit.

Monday · Retrieval Snapshot

Retrieve first; teach from what the learner shows

Monday uses short, low-stakes prompts across domains to identify secure knowledge and useful remediation targets.

Before hints

Give the learner a brief chance to retrieve independently through an accessible mode before coaching. Retrieval evidence can be speech, sign, AAC, object placement, drawing, pointing, model use, or dictation.

Three evidence labels

Adult notes may use secure, needs practice, and not yet observed. These are instructional routing labels—not grades, identities, permanent tracks, or public group names.

First-semester snapshot

  1. Retell or sequence a three-part story.
  2. Count/compare a small set and extend a repeating pattern.
  3. Describe a shape or spatial relationship.
  4. Separate observation from a guess in a science scenario.
  5. Use one privacy/safety/boundary rule from maps, animals, money, light, or community work.
  6. Identify one concept the learner wants to practice.

No score from the snapshot

Monday completion means the retrieval experience occurred. It does not create a percentage, award mastery, or predict a fixed outcome.

Route, don't label

If a concept is shaky, identify a prior week or concrete representation to revisit. If secure, use a new example that requires transfer rather than repetitive drilling.

Monday evidence checkpoint

Save after a cross-domain retrieval snapshot and at least one identified remediation or transfer target.

Teach · Practise · Correct · Transfer

Prerequisite retrieval: Retrieve the selected literacy target from the snapshot and check the prerequisite needed before practice.

Explicit teacher model: Use target → explicit model → think-aloud → guided example to teach one identified literacy subskill, such as story sequence, sound feature, or meaningful print.

Guided-to-independent practice: Practice with decreasing prompts across three examples, then give a fresh item with altered pictures/words so it is not memorized repetition. Keep each literacy subskill separately recorded.

Common misconception and correction: If a learner can copy a pattern but cannot identify its rule, model the rule explicitly. If print recognition is confused with sound knowledge, test and teach them separately.

Fresh transfer check: Use unfamiliar but comparable words/story pictures to check the same target. Ask the learner to explain, point, or sequence independently before giving any hint.

Evidence rule: Record the target skills separately and distinguish independent responses from prompted ones. Fresh transfer is collected before hints.

Diagnostic evidence & next teaching decision

Select one literacy skill from Monday's evidence. Teach the prerequisite explicitly, model a think-aloud, complete a guided example, and then give a new word/story/picture item without hints. Record what changed and which support helped.

Response status: Record each target as independent, prompted, or not yet observed. “Not yet observed” means more evidence or teaching is needed; it is not a label about the child.

Scoring boundary: This snapshot is instructional planning evidence only—not mastery credit.

Tuesday · Targeted Literacy & Language Lab

Retrieve meaning, word structure, and communication—not performance style

Children revisit first-semester literacy concepts using the areas revealed by Monday's snapshot.

Story and meaning

Sequence beginning/middle/end, retell important meaning without exact memorization, and support an idea with one story detail. A fictional/public story remains valid; personal family disclosure is not required.

Language patterns

Distinguish at least two of rhyme, syllable, beginning sound/feature, letter, or alliteration using an appropriate spoken, signed, visual, tactile, home-language, or other accessible pathway.

Target → model → try again → transfer

  1. Choose one literacy target from Monday.
  2. Adult models or re-teaches the concept explicitly.
  3. Learner tries a supported example.
  4. Learner tries a new example with reduced support.
  5. Explain/show what clue or rule helped.

Print/sound boundary

Review that letters and sounds are related but not perfectly one-to-one, rhyme depends on pronunciation, syllable count is not letter count, and accent/dialect differences are not errors simply because they differ from the teacher.

No fluency theater

Reading aloud fast, projecting a loud voice, eye contact, handwriting speed, memorized recitation, or public performance is not the literacy mastery target.

Tuesday evidence checkpoint

Save after targeted literacy remediation plus a transfer example showing what the learner can now retrieve/explain.

Teach · Practise · Correct · Transfer

Prerequisite retrieval: Retrieve the selected mathematics target and its concrete prerequisite, such as one-to-one counting or representing a quantity.

Explicit teacher model: Model a concrete representation, then connect it to drawings or numerals only where that representation has been taught. Think aloud about why the model matches the problem.

Guided-to-independent practice: Move concrete → picture → symbol as appropriate, include guided error correction, and finish with a new number/shape/measurement example with changed objects or numbers.

Common misconception and correction: If numeral naming is mistaken for cardinality, ask the learner to count and explain the total. If the same measurement uses unequal units, return to common units. If pattern continuation lacks a rule, rebuild the repeating unit.

Fresh transfer check: Present a fresh math problem using new objects/numbers and ask the learner to choose a representation, solve, and explain what the representation means.

Evidence rule: Record the target skills separately and distinguish independent responses from prompted ones. Fresh transfer is collected before hints.

Diagnostic evidence & next teaching decision

Select one mathematical skill from Monday's evidence. Use concrete material first, model why it represents the problem, guide two examples, then provide a changed-number or changed-object transfer item. Score counting, representation, and explanation separately.

Response status: Record each target as independent, prompted, or not yet observed. “Not yet observed” means more evidence or teaching is needed; it is not a label about the child.

Scoring boundary: This snapshot is instructional planning evidence only—not mastery credit.

Wednesday · Targeted Mathematics Lab

Represent the reasoning, then vary the example

Children retrieve and remediate first-semester number, pattern, geometry, comparison, and measurement ideas.

Number and quantity

Count with one-to-one correspondence, compare more/fewer/same, compose/decompose quantities to 5, and model a simple number story. Use objects, tactile counters, marks, fingers when appropriate, AAC, or drawings.

Pattern and geometry

Identify the repeating unit of an AB/AAB/ABB pattern; describe shapes by properties; use positional language; and compose shapes or interpret a simple map/model when useful.

Target → represent → explain → change

  1. Select one weak math target from Monday.
  2. Represent it concretely.
  3. Explain/show the relationship or rule.
  4. Change the numbers, orientation, object, unit, or context.
  5. Solve again and explain what stayed the same.

Measurement fairness

Review common starts, equal repeated units, number + unit, and error detection. Body size, stride, grip strength, mobility, or fine-motor skill are not measurement standards.

Economic math stays fictional

Token counting/comparison may be used, but no family money, allowance, possessions, savings, purchases, or ability to afford something is learner evidence.

Wednesday evidence checkpoint

Save after targeted math remediation and a changed-example transfer task with an explanation or accessible representation.

Teach · Practise · Correct · Transfer

Prerequisite retrieval: Retrieve a safe inquiry skill and ask the learner to identify what counts as evidence for the target question.

Explicit teacher model: Model an inquiry cycle: question, prediction, method, observation, evidence, cautious conclusion. Use supplied data/images where outdoor conditions or materials are unavailable.

Guided-to-independent practice: Practice with a simple data or model task; ask the learner to identify what changes and what stays the same, then interpret a fresh sample and explain one limitation.

Common misconception and correction: If prediction is recorded as observation, separate the labels. If one example is used to claim a universal rule, narrow the claim to the sample. If the activity is unsafe or inaccessible, switch to a model/picture pathway without changing the reasoning target.

Fresh transfer check: Use an unfamiliar but age-appropriate scenario requiring an observation, a count/comparison or model, and a cautious evidence-based explanation.

Evidence rule: Record the target skills separately and distinguish independent responses from prompted ones. Fresh transfer is collected before hints.

Diagnostic evidence & next teaching decision

Use one safe inquiry task with a clear question, method, evidence sample, and cautious conclusion. Teach observation versus prediction explicitly, then ask the learner to reason about a different supplied image/data sample without hints.

Response status: Record each target as independent, prompted, or not yet observed. “Not yet observed” means more evidence or teaching is needed; it is not a label about the child.

Scoring boundary: This snapshot is instructional planning evidence only—not mastery credit.

Thursday · Inquiry & Integrated Demonstration Lab

Use evidence, models, safety, and revision across domains

Thursday combines previously learned inquiry skills without copying a future milestone prompt.

Evidence habits

Choose among observation vs inference, prediction vs result, classification rule, model limitation, fair comparison, or evidence-based revision. The goal is to explain why the evidence supports a claim.

Transfer across topics

Examples can draw from weather, plants, animals/habitats, maps, light/shadows, community care, engineering, or needs/choices. Use a new scenario so the learner must transfer—not memorize—a response.

Integrated practice demonstration

  1. Observe or inspect a provided safe model/scenario.
  2. Name the question or goal.
  3. Use one math representation, comparison, count, pattern, or measurement where relevant.
  4. Use evidence to explain one science/community/design idea.
  5. Name a safety, privacy, dignity, or model-limit boundary.
  6. Revise one idea when new evidence is introduced.

Practice is not the milestone

Practice should use analogous skill families, not rehearsed copies of a future demonstration. Do not turn review into answer rehearsal or coach the learner toward a memorized item order.

Accommodations preserve the target

Changing the response mode, sensory format, material size, positioning, language access, or amount of processing time can remove barriers while preserving the reasoning target.

Thursday evidence checkpoint

Save after an integrated transfer task using evidence plus at least one appropriate math/inquiry/boundary idea and one revision or limitation.

Teach · Practise · Correct · Transfer

Prerequisite retrieval: Retrieve independently the focus skills selected from the snapshot and identify which supports are available without giving away the answer.

Explicit teacher model: Model how to explain a strategy and how to revise an answer from evidence, while keeping the final transfer prompt unseen until the child is ready.

Guided-to-independent practice: Use short guided repair tasks, then complete a fresh integrated task that combines a literacy response, one math representation, and an inquiry evidence claim. Score strands separately.

Common misconception and correction: If a combined task hides which strand is difficult, separate and recheck each component. If support materially changed the skill being measured, record the support and provide a parallel accessible task.

Fresh transfer check: Use a novel integrated scenario with a short story/picture, a small concrete quantity, and one observation/data clue. Ask the learner to respond independently, explain one strategy, and identify a next goal. Treat readiness as planning evidence, not a fixed label.

Evidence rule: Record the target skills separately and distinguish independent responses from prompted ones. Fresh transfer is collected before hints.

Diagnostic evidence & next teaching decision

Compare the initial diagnostic with the fresh parallel task for each strand. Record independent transfer, transfer after support, and not-yet separately. Choose the next instructional target; do not pass/fail the child or substitute this week for the formal midyear gate.

Response status: Record each target as independent, prompted, or not yet observed. “Not yet observed” means more evidence or teaching is needed; it is not a label about the child.

Scoring boundary: This snapshot is instructional planning evidence only—not mastery credit.

Friday · Readiness & Growth Conference

Revisit weak spots, select evidence, and explain the next step

Friday closes the instructional week; it does not substitute for Week 18 mastery or the midyear demonstration.

Second retrieval

Return to one or two Monday targets with fresh examples. Look for improved strategy use, explanation, correction, or independence—not perfect performance or speed.

Error analysis

Errors are information. Learner or adult identifies what went wrong: misunderstood rule, lost count, weak representation, missing evidence, unsafe assumption, mixed unit, vocabulary confusion, or insufficient information. Then choose a repair.

Readiness conference

  1. Name one skill that is more secure now.
  2. Show one corrected idea or improved strategy.
  3. Select one work sample/model/drawing or dictated explanation as portfolio evidence.
  4. Name one area that may still need practice.
  5. Explain the sequence: Week 18 mastery first; midyear demonstration only after eligibility.

Celebration without ranking

Celebrate persistence, curiosity, repair, communication, and growth whether mastery is earned on the first attempt or after additional practice. No public scores, class rank, fastest-finisher award, or “passed/failed” display.
