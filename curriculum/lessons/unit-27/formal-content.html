Monday: Market Number Detectives

Daily learning target: Model a market story using up to ten counters, name how many items there are altogether, and explain one way to split the collection into two parts.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How do numbers help us solve real problems?”

Literacy: Story problem language

Math: Compose and decompose to 10

Inquiry: Community economy and fair exchange

SEL: Patience and sharing resources

Original guided activity: Set up a pretend market using ten paper fruit cards and reusable counters. Tell a short original story: “Our stand has three apples and two pears.” Children build the two groups, count all five objects once, and rearrange the same five into another pair of groups. Ask “What changed? What stayed the same?” Use drawing, pointing, AAC, or objects for responses.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner represents a story total correctly and demonstrates one valid decomposition of a number up to ten. Offer a smaller collection for support or two decompositions as an extension.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Tuesday: Ten Ways to Fill a Market Basket

Daily learning target: Compose and decompose quantities up to ten using two groups, represent one quantity in two different ways, and explain how both representations have the same total.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How do numbers help us solve real problems?”

Literacy: Story problem language

Math: Compose and decompose to 10

Inquiry: Community economy and fair exchange

SEL: Patience and sharing resources

Original guided activity: Draw two market baskets and prepare ten reusable paper produce counters. Give each child a target number from three to ten. Place some counters in the first basket and the rest in the second; say or show “four and three make seven.” Rearrange into a different valid pair without changing the total. Use a five-frame for support and invite an additional decomposition as an extension. Model that moving a counter between baskets changes the parts but not the whole.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner makes a target number with two groups, demonstrates a second valid split, and communicates that the whole remains unchanged through words, pointing, drawing, or an accessible device. An incorrect attempt receives a new example and guidance rather than restarting the lesson.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Wednesday: Fair Market Investigators

Daily learning target: Solve a pretend-market sharing problem with up to ten objects, compare two fair distributions, and explain what makes a share equal or unequal.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How do numbers help us solve real problems?”

Literacy: Story problem language

Math: Compose and decompose to 10

Inquiry: Community economy and fair exchange

SEL: Patience and sharing resources

Original guided activity: Use eight handmade produce cards and two paper market stalls. Ask children to distribute the cards so both stalls have the same number. Count each stall, check by pairing objects one-to-one, and try an unequal arrangement to explain the difference. Repeat using six cards or ten cards. Explain that equal sharing describes numbers of objects here, not the value or worth of people or their work.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner makes an equal distribution, verifies both groups by counting or matching, and explains why an unequal example differs. Support with four objects and visual pairing; extend by asking whether an odd total can be split equally into two whole-object groups.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Thursday: My Market Number Story Studio

Daily learning target: Create an original market story with a total of up to ten objects, represent its two parts using objects or pictures, and explain how the parts make the whole.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How do numbers help us solve real problems?”

Literacy: Story problem language

Math: Compose and decompose to 10

Inquiry: Community economy and fair exchange

SEL: Patience and sharing resources

Original guided activity: Each child invents a pretend market stand with two kinds of goods, using reusable paper cards, drawn shapes, or safe manipulatives. Choose a total from three to ten; divide it into two groups and illustrate the story on a folded paper market page. An adult may transcribe the child’s words. Exchange stories with a partner, who models and checks the total, then revise one detail while keeping the mathematics correct. Never require real money or purchases.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Child produces an original number story, shows two parts and a mathematically correct total, and communicates how the representation matches the story. Support with a five-frame or tactile counters; extend by making a second story with the same total and different parts. Incorrect attempts receive targeted feedback without restarting the lesson.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Friday: Market Mathematicians Reflection Circle

Daily learning target: Solve a new number story using up to ten objects, explain how two parts make a whole, and identify one strategy that helped when a problem was challenging.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How do numbers help us solve real problems?”

Literacy: Story problem language

Math: Compose and decompose to 10

Inquiry: Community economy and fair exchange

SEL: Patience and sharing resources

Original guided activity: Present a fresh market story different from the examples used earlier this week: “There are six oranges at the stand. Two are placed in one basket. How many are in the other basket?” Invite children to model with counters, pictures, or a five- or ten-frame, then discuss how to verify that two and four make six. Offer another unseen example within ten if needed, reteaching with concrete objects rather than repeating the same answer. Children choose their Thursday market page or a new drawing for a small learning portfolio and explain what improved.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Child shows a correct part-whole solution for a new story and identifies a helpful strategy by speaking, pointing, drawing, or accessible communication. Provide a number-to-five variation for guided support and a two-solution challenge for extension. An adult may then open the existing separate weekly mastery assessment; Friday participation or a portfolio entry never automatically awards 80% mastery or a certificate.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.
