Teacher / Family Learning Plan

A real five-day learning arc

Each day has a different purpose and produces its own learning evidence. Daily evidence unlocks the next day; it does not count as mastery. Friday opens the adult-observed mastery rubric only after all five learning experiences are complete.

Garden Connection: Week 4 developed the five senses. This week, children reuse those observation habits to notice color, shape, texture, sound, temperature, weather, and change in the natural world.

Day 1 · Launch + Observe

Monday · Nature Detectives

Objective: Observe and describe natural objects or images using specific words, identify the beginning sound in leaf, and record one weather observation.

Specific materials
2–5 leaves or leaf imagespapercrayon/penciloptional magnifier

Teacher model

  1. Think aloud: “I notice this leaf is green, smooth, and pointed.”
  2. Separate observation from guessing: “I can see green. I do not know why it fell yet.”
  3. Stretch the word leaf: “llll-eaf. I hear /l/ first.”
  4. Model a simple weather statement: “Today feels warm and the sky looks cloudy.”
Movement + language

Move like a leaf

Children stand, float slowly, spin, land, and freeze. Ask: “What movement word describes your leaf?” Accept speech, gesture, sign, home-language words, AAC, or drawing.

Scaffold: offer two choices—“smooth or bumpy?”, “light or dark?”
Extension: ask the child to compare two leaves using same and different.

Day 2 · Guided Skill Building

Tuesday · Pattern Garden

Objective: Create, extend, and explain repeating patterns using color, shape, movement, or objects while reusing Monday’s nature vocabulary.

Specific materials
leaves/paper shapes2 crayon colorscounters/buttonspaper strip

Teacher model

  1. Build an AB pattern: green, brown, green, brown…
  2. Name the repeating unit: “green-brown repeats.”
  3. Hide the next item and ask the child to predict it before revealing.
  4. Change the material but keep the rule: clap-stomp, clap-stomp.
Garden connection

Use yesterday’s details

Ask the child to build a pattern using two features noticed Monday: light/dark, smooth/bumpy, big/small, or two colors.

Scaffold: begin with AB.
Extension: try AAB or ABB and ask the child to explain the repeating unit.

Day 3 · Inquiry + Evidence

Wednesday · Season Scientists

Objective: Compare seasonal changes, connect weather to observations, and make a simple prediction using evidence.

Specific materials
season pictures OR drawingsMonday observation4 paper season cards

Teacher model

  1. Compare two scenes: “In one picture the tree has many green leaves; in another it has fewer brown leaves.”
  2. Use evidence language: “I think the season changed because I notice…”
  3. Explain that seasons look different in different parts of the world.
  4. Invite local knowledge: rainy/dry seasons, snow/no snow, evergreen trees, tropical climates.
Universal geography

No single-season stereotype

Children may describe the seasonal patterns where they actually live. A Florida, Ghanaian, Brazilian, Canadian, Indian, or Australian learner should not be forced into the same weather script.

Extension: compare your local pattern with another place.
Scaffold: use “I notice ___, so I think ___.”

Day 4 · Create + Communicate

Thursday · Leaf Studio

Objective: Create a visual or tactile artifact that combines nature observation and a repeating pattern, then explain one artistic and one scientific choice.

Specific materials
fallen leaf OR textured object/photopapercrayons/pencilsreused paper stripsafe glue/tape optional

Studio sequence

  1. Make a leaf rubbing, texture drawing, traced leaf, or observational drawing.
  2. Create an AB, AAB, or ABB pattern on a crown, strip, border, necklace, or digital-free paper design.
  3. Add one nature word or beginning-sound label.
  4. Explain: “My pattern repeats ___.”
  5. Explain: “One change I noticed in nature is ___.”
Choice + inclusion

One goal, many forms

The artifact may be drawn, built, arranged, collaged, tactile, dictated to an adult, photographed locally, or presented orally. Fine-motor difficulty must never erase conceptual understanding.

Family connection: take a short “pattern walk” at home. Find a repeating pattern in fabric, tiles, music, steps, windows, plants, or household objects.

Day 5 · Retrieval + Remediation

Friday · Remember, Explain & Reflect

Objective: Retrieve the week's most important ideas without notes, identify anything that needs another try, and prepare for the adult-observed mastery demonstration.

Before mastery

Retrieval first, assessment second

This practice is intentionally not the formal mastery score. If the learner misses something, revisit the matching day, use a Mentor hint, manipulate real objects, and try again. Friday is a chance to repair understanding before formal evidence is recorded.

Reflection

Growth question

Ask: “What can you do now that was harder on Monday?” Accept a spoken answer, drawing, gesture, demonstration, dictated sentence, or home-language response.

🌟 Five Garden Retrievals

1. Which word begins with the /l/ sound?

2. What comes next: 🍁 🍃 🍁 🍃 __?

3. Which sentence is an observation?

4. A repeating pattern has…

5. Seasons and weather…

Try all five. Four correct shows you are ready to move to the reflection step; you may retry as often as needed.
