Monday: Wonder Launch

Daily learning target: Choose a focused, investigable question, share what is already known, and identify one thing still to find out.

Recall & vocabulary: Recall something you wonder about. Key words: question, known, unknown, investigable (something we can explore safely), evidence.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “What big question do I want to explore?”

Literacy: Question words and oral presentation

Math: Choose math tools for a project

Inquiry: Inquiry, evidence, design, and communication

SEL: Confidence and collaboration

Workshop: Use a wonder board with picture prompts. Each learner selects or dictates one question that can be explored safely with classroom materials or supplied images. Sort cards into “what I know,” “what I wonder,” and “what I need to find out”; help narrow questions that are too broad.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Teach · Practice · Show independently

Adult modeling: Ask “Which of these paper towers is taller?” beside a picture of two block towers. Say: “I can investigate by counting equal-size blocks. I know both are towers; I still need their heights.” Contrast with “Why is everything beautiful?” which needs narrowing to an observable question.

Guided practice (three varied examples):

  1. Sort “How many red paper squares are here?” into safely testable; explain what to count.
  2. Narrow “How do plants grow?” to “Which of these illustrated seedlings has more leaves?” when only pictures are available.
  3. Use a wonder board to place “I know both groups have buttons” apart from “I wonder which group has more.”

Fresh independent transfer: Give fresh picture prompts showing two leaf collections and three drawn ramps. Without an adult selecting it, formulate one safe, answerable comparison question, state what is known, and identify missing evidence.

Corrective feedback: If a question cannot be tested with available materials, preserve the child's curiosity while helping identify an observable and manageable part of it.

Accessible evidence: Accept speech, drawing, tactile cards, pointing, dictated words, or supported communication as appropriate. Record the actual question, procedure, observation, artifact, or explanation, not simply an adult-completed worksheet. Daily practice is separate from the adult-observed 80% mastery decision.

Tuesday: Guided Skill Lab

Daily learning target: Plan a safe way to investigate the question, choose a useful counting, sorting, measuring, or recording tool, and explain the plan.

Recall & vocabulary: Recall a question that can be answered by counting, sorting, or observing. Key words: plan, material, method, safe, source.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “What big question do I want to explore?”

Literacy: Question words and oral presentation

Math: Choose math tools for a project

Inquiry: Inquiry, evidence, design, and communication

SEL: Confidence and collaboration

Workshop: Learners choose a method card—observe, sort, count, compare, measure with equal units, or build a model—and select a matching tool. Make a three-picture plan: first gather evidence, next record it, last explain what it may show. Adult checks safety and feasibility before the investigation.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Teach · Practice · Show independently

Adult modeling: For the question “Which of two paper strips is longer?”, choose identical paper squares as nonstandard units. Model lining them end-to-end with no gaps, recording counts, and stating that units must be equal.

Guided practice (three varied examples):

  1. Match “Which group has more?” with counters or tally marks.
  2. Match “What shapes appear?” with a sorting mat and shape cards.
  3. Order three picture-plan cards: gather evidence, record results, explain what they might mean.

Fresh independent transfer: Given a new question comparing numbers of shapes in two drawings, independently choose an appropriate counting or sorting tool, put three investigation steps in order, and explain how to keep the comparison fair.

Corrective feedback: If the child chooses differently sized units to measure lengths, demonstrate how unequal units distort comparisons and offer a new equal-unit example.

Accessible evidence: Accept speech, drawing, tactile cards, pointing, dictated words, or supported communication as appropriate. Record the actual question, procedure, observation, artifact, or explanation, not simply an adult-completed worksheet. Daily practice is separate from the adult-observed 80% mastery decision.

Wednesday: Inquiry Workshop

Daily learning target: Gather or examine evidence for the question, record at least two observations, and distinguish a noticed detail from a guess.

Recall & vocabulary: Recall how to describe what you actually see. Key words: observe, record, compare, result, prediction.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “What big question do I want to explore?”

Literacy: Question words and oral presentation

Math: Choose math tools for a project

Inquiry: Inquiry, evidence, design, and communication

SEL: Confidence and collaboration

Workshop: Carry out the planned investigation with a small, safe sample or provided pictures. Record at least two observations with drawings, marks, labels, tallies, dictation, or AAC. Use two sentence frames: “I noticed…” and “I think…” so observation and inference stay distinct.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Teach · Practice · Show independently

Adult modeling: Study an original drawing with four green leaves and two yellow leaves. Say “I notice four green leaves” and mark four tallies. Say “The plant is healthy” is a guess, not something this drawing proves.

Guided practice (three varied examples):

  1. Count the objects in a new picture and record their number with matching tallies.
  2. Compare a second picture; identify one directly visible detail without guessing a cause.
  3. Use “I noticed…” and “I think…” sentence frames to separate observation and inference.

Fresh independent transfer: Provide an unfamiliar original picture of two small seed trays containing three and five pictured seedlings. Without a model answer, record two verifiable observations, compare the counts, and offer one clearly labeled prediction.

Corrective feedback: If an observation incorrectly counts an object, point to each object once with a counter, invite recounting, and retain the earlier attempt as evidence of learning.

Accessible evidence: Accept speech, drawing, tactile cards, pointing, dictated words, or supported communication as appropriate. Record the actual question, procedure, observation, artifact, or explanation, not simply an adult-completed worksheet. Daily practice is separate from the adult-observed 80% mastery decision.

Thursday: Create & Communicate

Daily learning target: Create a model, picture, demonstration, or artifact that communicates the investigation, then revise one feature using evidence or feedback.

Recall & vocabulary: Recall a time a picture, graph, or model helped explain an idea. Key words: model, graph, label, represent, revise.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “What big question do I want to explore?”

Literacy: Question words and oral presentation

Math: Choose math tools for a project

Inquiry: Inquiry, evidence, design, and communication

SEL: Confidence and collaboration

Workshop: Learners choose a way to communicate their result: labeled drawing, model, simple graph, sequence, demonstration, or dictated explanation. Ask what the evidence actually supports, invite one kind peer question, then revise one feature or explain why no revision is needed.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Teach · Practice · Show independently

Adult modeling: Create a simple two-column picture graph of four round shapes and two triangles. Label each column, explain “four is two more than two,” and revise one missing label after a pretend partner asks what the bars mean.

Guided practice (three varied examples):

  1. Choose a drawing, graph, or model that directly answers a sample counting question.
  2. Use two observations to support one specific statement, without claiming proof of something not measured.
  3. Respond to a constructive question by updating a label, comparison, or source of evidence.

Fresh independent transfer: Using a fresh collection of six mixed shape pictures, independently create a labeled visual representation, state one evidence-backed result, and make one meaningful revision after a neutral clarification question.

Corrective feedback: If the learner's claim exceeds the evidence, ask “Which mark or picture shows that?” and support a narrower justified statement.

Accessible evidence: Accept speech, drawing, tactile cards, pointing, dictated words, or supported communication as appropriate. Record the actual question, procedure, observation, artifact, or explanation, not simply an adult-completed worksheet. Daily practice is separate from the adult-observed 80% mastery decision.

Friday: Review, Assessment & Reflection

Daily learning target: Share a finding or uncertainty, connect it to a piece of evidence, select a portfolio sample, and pose one next question.

Recall & vocabulary: Recall your project question and one observation. Key words: finding, evidence, uncertainty, conclusion, next question.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “What big question do I want to explore?”

Literacy: Question words and oral presentation

Math: Choose math tools for a project

Inquiry: Inquiry, evidence, design, and communication

SEL: Confidence and collaboration

Workshop: Invite the learner to present or privately explain the question, method, two evidence points, result or uncertainty, and a next question. Select one artifact for the portfolio and record the learner's reflection. Sharing aloud is optional; pointing, AAC, dictation, or a teacher-mediated demonstration counts.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Teach · Practice · Show independently

Adult modeling: Present the question “Which paper stack has more shapes?” with two drawn groups of three and five, the learner's counts, and a simple tally page. Model: “I counted three and five; five is more. I would next wonder whether a different group has the same pattern.”

Guided practice (three varied examples):

  1. Practice privately explaining a question, method, and one observation using three picture cards.
  2. Check a result against two recorded counts or details and identify what remains unknown.
  3. Choose a portfolio sample that genuinely demonstrates the investigation and formulate one next question.

Fresh independent transfer: With an unfamiliar miniature investigation of two pictured button groups, independently identify a question, describe how counting would answer it, cite two observed totals, communicate a defensible conclusion or uncertainty, and pose a next question.

Corrective feedback: If the learner confidently asserts something beyond the recorded evidence, affirm the curiosity and help distinguish a supported finding from a question for another investigation.

Accessible evidence: Accept speech, drawing, tactile cards, pointing, dictated words, or supported communication as appropriate. Record the actual question, procedure, observation, artifact, or explanation, not simply an adult-completed worksheet. Daily practice is separate from the adult-observed 80% mastery decision.
