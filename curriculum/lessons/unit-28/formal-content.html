Monday: What Helps Our Bodies Grow?

Daily learning target: Identify at least three everyday body needs, such as food, water, sleep, movement, and care, and explain one practical way to meet a need.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How do we care for our bodies?”

Literacy: Health vocabulary

Math: Sort food groups/colors and count healthy choices

Inquiry: Body needs and healthy habits

SEL: Self-care

Original guided activity: Make five original picture cards showing water, a meal, sleep, play or movement, and getting help when unwell. Invite learners to match each card to a simple body need and describe how it helps. Read a short educator-created story about a child preparing for a busy day; pause for predictions about eating, drinking, resting, and asking for help. Emphasize that families eat different foods, bodies have different abilities, and no food or body is labeled good or bad. Offer pointing, drawing, speech, or accessible communication.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner identifies three body needs and communicates one safe self-care example. Support with two cards and adult modeling; extend by comparing how needs may be met in different families. Record observations without collecting medical details or judging food access.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Tuesday: Food Colors and Sorting Detectives

Daily learning target: Sort eight illustrated food cards using a stated observable attribute, count each group accurately, and explain how a different sorting rule changes the groups.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How do we care for our bodies?”

Literacy: Health vocabulary

Math: Sort food groups/colors and count healthy choices

Inquiry: Body needs and healthy habits

SEL: Self-care

Original guided activity: Create eight original picture cards showing familiar and culturally varied foods, including a fruit, vegetable, grain, bean or other protein food, and a meal combining ingredients. First sort by a visible color or shape and count each set by touching one card at a time. Then invite learners to change the rule, such as foods that grow on plants or foods that combine ingredients; discuss why a mixed dish may belong in more than one descriptive group. Treat foods without moral labels and never ask children to disclose what their families can afford or eat.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Child sorts eight cards consistently, accurately counts a chosen group, and communicates the sorting rule through speech, pointing, drawing, or accessible communication. Support with four cards and two contrasting colors; extend by comparing two valid rules and explaining why counts change.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.
