Monday: What Helps Our Bodies Grow?

Daily learning target: Identify at least three everyday body needs, such as food, water, sleep, movement, and care, and explain one practical way to meet a need.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How do we care for our bodies?”

Literacy: Health vocabulary

Math: Sort food groups/colors and count healthy choices

Inquiry: Body needs and healthy habits

SEL: Self-care

Original guided activity: Make five original picture cards showing water, a meal, sleep, play or movement, and getting help when unwell. Invite learners to match each card to a simple body need and describe how it helps. Read a short educator-created story about a child preparing for a busy day; pause for predictions about eating, drinking, resting, and asking for help. Emphasize that families eat different foods, bodies have different abilities, and no food or body is labeled good or bad. Offer pointing, drawing, speech, or accessible communication.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner identifies three body needs and communicates one safe self-care example. Support with two cards and adult modeling; extend by comparing how needs may be met in different families. Record observations without collecting medical details or judging food access.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Tuesday: Food Colors and Sorting Detectives

Daily learning target: Sort eight illustrated food cards using a stated observable attribute, count each group accurately, and explain how a different sorting rule changes the groups.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How do we care for our bodies?”

Literacy: Health vocabulary

Math: Sort food groups/colors and count healthy choices

Inquiry: Body needs and healthy habits

SEL: Self-care

Original guided activity: Create eight original picture cards showing familiar and culturally varied foods, including a fruit, vegetable, grain, bean or other protein food, and a meal combining ingredients. First sort by a visible color or shape and count each set by touching one card at a time. Then invite learners to change the rule, such as foods that grow on plants or foods that combine ingredients; discuss why a mixed dish may belong in more than one descriptive group. Treat foods without moral labels and never ask children to disclose what their families can afford or eat.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Child sorts eight cards consistently, accurately counts a chosen group, and communicates the sorting rule through speech, pointing, drawing, or accessible communication. Support with four cards and two contrasting colors; extend by comparing two valid rules and explaining why counts change.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Wednesday: Body Needs Investigation

Daily learning target: Match water, rest, movement, and asking for care to everyday situations, and explain two ways people can respond safely to a body need.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How do we care for our bodies?”

Literacy: Health vocabulary

Math: Sort food groups/colors and count healthy choices

Inquiry: Body needs and healthy habits

SEL: Self-care

Original guided activity: Use four original situation cards: after active play, after a busy day, before outdoor movement, and when someone feels unwell. Provide matching picture cards for drinking water, taking a rest, choosing comfortable movement, and asking a trusted adult for help. Children predict a helpful response, match cards, and explain why choices can differ from person to person. Invite a brief voluntary stretch or seated movement with a quiet observation alternative. Do not withhold drinking water, impose exercise, or collect personal medical information.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner selects an appropriate response for two everyday scenarios and explains or shows one reason through words, pictures, gestures, or an accessible communication device. Support with two scenarios and modeling; extend by comparing two reasonable responses to the same situation.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Thursday: My Caring-Day Story Studio

Daily learning target: Create a three-scene picture story that shows at least two practical self-care choices, and explain how those choices help meet everyday body needs.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How do we care for our bodies?”

Literacy: Health vocabulary

Math: Sort food groups/colors and count healthy choices

Inquiry: Body needs and healthy habits

SEL: Self-care

Original guided activity: Fold one sheet of paper into three picture panels labeled morning, daytime, and evening. Invite each child to invent an original character and illustrate choices such as drinking water, eating a meal, playing or moving comfortably, resting, or asking a trusted adult for help. Help learners sequence their story using first, next, and last; adults may transcribe dictated captions. Use drawings, collage, tactile pieces, or spoken storytelling. Welcome different family schedules, foods, cultures, and abilities without labeling bodies or meals as good or bad.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner presents three ordered scenes, identifies two distinct self-care choices, and communicates how one supports a body need. Support with pre-drawn blank panels and two choice cards; extend by comparing two reasonable caring-day routines. The learner may share privately rather than perform for a group, and no personal health information is requested.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Friday: Caring Choices Reflection Garden

Daily learning target: Apply two everyday self-care ideas to unfamiliar picture scenarios, explain one reason for a choice, and select a learning example to reflect on growth.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How do we care for our bodies?”

Literacy: Health vocabulary

Math: Sort food groups/colors and count healthy choices

Inquiry: Body needs and healthy habits

SEL: Self-care

Original guided activity: Present two new educator-drawn scenes: a child returning from outdoor play who may want water and a rest, and a child feeling unwell who needs support from a trusted adult. Invite children to choose among original picture cards and explain which caring responses fit each situation; allow more than one reasonable response. Then review a fresh collection of four food images, sort them by a visible property, and count one group without assigning moral labels to foods. Learners choose a scene or their Thursday three-panel story for a portfolio and tell what they learned. When a choice needs correction, provide a new concrete example and gentle modeling rather than repeating identical questions.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner matches helpful responses to two new situations, explains one choice, and demonstrates a consistent sort and accurate count with picture cards. Provide two-option cards and adult modeling when useful; extend by describing two different ways to meet a need. Reflection and participation do not automatically grant 80% mastery, formal assessment credit, or a certificate; use the separately linked assessment when appropriate. Do not request personal medical information.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.
