Teach · Practise · Correct · Transfer

Prerequisite retrieval: Retrieve Week 7 positional language and Week 8 movement-pattern experience. Invite the learner to choose a safe mode—seated, tabletop, paper token, or body movement.

Explicit teacher model: Model action words and position words separately: hop/roll are actions; above/below/beside/forward describe action or location. Show that bodies move in different ways and the task target does not require a particular range of motion.

Guided-to-independent practice: Follow a short three-step path with guidance, then describe a different path using position words. Build an AB movement or symbol pattern with objects or gestures and identify its unit.

Common misconception and correction: If directions are unclear, add a named reference point and starting orientation. If movement ability or speed becomes the score, offer seated or token-based equivalence and assess the language/pattern target instead.

Fresh transfer check: Use a new grid and a new start orientation. Ask the learner to follow or describe three positional steps, then extend a novel repeating action/symbol pattern and explain the unit.

Evidence rule: Score spatial/pattern concepts separately from performance style. A fresh transfer item uses new directions, objects, or score symbols before hints.

Monday: Movement Words and Body Explorers

Daily learning target: Demonstrate or identify three action verbs and describe how joints and muscles help people move, using an accessible movement or visual model.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How does movement help us learn?”

Literacy: Action verbs

Math: Position, direction, and movement patterns

Inquiry: Body movement, bones/muscles basics

SEL: Confidence and safe risk

Original guided activity: Introduce three original action cards labeled bend, reach, and turn. Invite learners to model each action safely with a hand, arm, whole body, picture pointer, or movable paper figure. Compare a straight paper strip with a paper figure joined at an elbow-like hinge, and notice where bending is possible. Explain simply that muscles help move body parts and joints are places where bones meet. Avoid forced balancing, stretching, or assumptions that every body moves the same way. Ask children to describe a movement using an action word.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner correctly identifies or represents three action verbs, points to or labels a bending joint on a model, and communicates one observation about movement. Support with two cards and adult modeling; extend by sequencing two safe movements using first and next. Participation may be seated, nonverbal, or entirely model-based.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Teach · Practise · Correct · Transfer

Prerequisite retrieval: Retrieve positional language and an AB pattern rule from previous lessons.

Explicit teacher model: Model a direction card sequence and an AB pattern. Demonstrate how the same directions change meaning if the starting orientation changes; mark start and finish clearly.

Guided-to-independent practice: Learner follows one guided route, then creates a route card for a partner. Extend a new pattern and check whether the partner can follow it accurately.

Common misconception and correction: If left/right is confused after turning, reset orientation and use an arrow/compass card. If pattern sequence is merely a list of actions without repeat, locate the repeating unit explicitly.

Fresh transfer check: Give a fresh grid with changed starting direction and a new movement/symbol pattern. Ask learner to explain the route and continue the pattern with no model visible.

Evidence rule: Score spatial/pattern concepts separately from performance style. A fresh transfer item uses new directions, objects, or score symbols before hints.

Tuesday: Direction and Pattern Pathways

Daily learning target: Use at least three positional words accurately and create, extend, and explain an AB movement or symbol pattern using a safe, accessible pathway.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How does movement help us learn?”

Literacy: Action verbs

Math: Position, direction, and movement patterns

Inquiry: Body movement, bones/muscles basics

SEL: Confidence and safe risk

Original guided activity: Draw a simple path with paper arrows and two kinds of symbol cards. Model above, below, beside, and between using a movable paper character and familiar classroom objects. Create a repeating pattern such as reach, tap, reach, tap; invite children to predict what comes next and explain the two-action unit. Learners may use safe seated gestures, place cards, point, or guide the paper character instead of moving their bodies. Change one position and ask children to describe what changed without requiring left-right mastery.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner shows or describes three positions and accurately continues a repeating AB pattern, identifying its repeated pair through speech, gesture, drawing, or accessible communication. Support with two positions and two repeated pairs; extend with an AAB pattern or a new paper pathway. Do not require balancing, standing, or timed physical performance.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Teach · Practise · Correct · Transfer

Prerequisite retrieval: Retrieve the meaning of balance/stability in a model rather than asking for a physical balancing performance.

Explicit teacher model: Model a paper figure or block structure with a support point. Change one feature at a time, observe whether it stays upright, and explain that the model represents selected aspects of body movement.

Guided-to-independent practice: Test two paper or tabletop models with guidance, then independently change one support/hinge and describe the observed effect. Label a body structure only when the model supports the relationship.

Common misconception and correction: If appearance is treated as proof of internal anatomy, clarify a model's limits. If stability is equated with body ability or learner worth, return to the model task and offer an accessible response mode.

Fresh transfer check: Present a new paper-figure design with a different support position. Ask what may happen, identify one testable change, and explain what the result does and does not show.

Evidence rule: Score spatial/pattern concepts separately from performance style. A fresh transfer item uses new directions, objects, or score symbols before hints.

Wednesday: Balance and Body-Model Detectives

Daily learning target: Investigate how a model stays balanced on a support, describe one change that makes it more stable, and identify a joint that allows a paper figure to bend.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How does movement help us learn?”

Literacy: Action verbs

Math: Position, direction, and movement patterns

Inquiry: Body movement, bones/muscles basics

SEL: Confidence and safe risk

Original guided activity: Create a paper figure with a folded hinge at an elbow and a separate wide-based paper stand. Place a lightweight paper shape over the stand, observe whether it tips, then widen or reposition the support and test again. Ask children to predict, observe, and compare one change at a time. Use a stable table and light paper pieces only; this activity does not require children to balance on their bodies or use unsafe objects. Explain that our paper model demonstrates support and bending but does not fully explain how real human balance works.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner describes which paper setup stays upright, explains one change to the support, and points to or identifies the figure’s bending hinge. Offer pre-folded models and picture choices for support; extend by comparing two safe support widths. Responses may be spoken, drawn, pointed to, or communicated through an accessible device.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Teach · Practise · Correct · Transfer

Prerequisite retrieval: Retrieve positional directions, pattern rules, and safe testing habits.

Explicit teacher model: Model planning a pathway with three directions, one repeating sequence, and a clear safety/access constraint. Show a test, note one ambiguity, and revise the instruction rather than blaming the follower.

Guided-to-independent practice: Learner designs a route/pattern on paper, object grid, or accessible movement form. Another person tries it; learner revises one confusing instruction and checks the pattern's unit.

Common misconception and correction: If the route has multiple interpretations, add a start marker/reference direction. If a design assumes everyone moves the same way, add a choice of equivalent route/action while keeping the learning goal constant.

Fresh transfer check: Give a new pathway constraint and a new pattern rule. Ask for an independent design and one explanation of how feedback/evidence supports a revision.

Evidence rule: Score spatial/pattern concepts separately from performance style. A fresh transfer item uses new directions, objects, or score symbols before hints.

Thursday: My Movement Path Design Studio

Daily learning target: Design and revise a paper movement pathway containing three positional directions and one repeating pattern, then explain a purposeful change to the design.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How does movement help us learn?”

Literacy: Action verbs

Math: Position, direction, and movement patterns

Inquiry: Body movement, bones/muscles basics

SEL: Confidence and safe risk

Original guided activity: Give each learner a blank paper pathway, a paper character, and handmade arrows or shape cards. Build a safe route with three location clues, such as go beside the tree, move between two squares, and stop below the sun. Add an AB pattern of symbols along the path. Ask a partner or adult to follow the directions using the paper character, notice any confusing instruction, and revise one clue. Learners may direct the route with pointing, drawing, speech, or accessible communication; no physical obstacle course or standing is required.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Child shows three understandable position clues, extends an AB symbol pattern correctly, and explains one revision made after testing. Support with two destination cards and a pre-drawn route; extend by designing two valid routes or a more complex AAB pattern. Keep the finished paper map as an optional learning portfolio example.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Teach · Practise · Correct · Transfer

Prerequisite retrieval: Retrieve action/position words, repeating unit, and model-versus-real-world distinction without a worked example.

Explicit teacher model: Model an explanation connecting one route instruction, one pattern rule, and one observed model result. State what can be supported by evidence and what remains outside the model.

Guided-to-independent practice: Use guided retrieval with feedback, then a new grid/figure scenario. Score spatial language, pattern reasoning, and model interpretation separately.

Common misconception and correction: If the integrated result hides which subskill is weak, isolate positional language, pattern rule, or model inference and reteach that part only.

Fresh transfer check: Use an unseen route with a changed start orientation and a fresh support model. Ask the learner to describe the route, extend the pattern, and name one model limitation independently.

Evidence rule: Score spatial/pattern concepts separately from performance style. A fresh transfer item uses new directions, objects, or score symbols before hints.

Friday: Movement Scientists Reflection Circle

Daily learning target: Interpret a new paper pathway using three positional clues, extend a repeating movement-symbol pattern, and explain one observation about a stable paper model.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How does movement help us learn?”

Literacy: Action verbs

Math: Position, direction, and movement patterns

Inquiry: Body movement, bones/muscles basics

SEL: Confidence and safe risk

Original guided activity: Present a new educator-drawn paper map with a character beside a tree, a bridge between two blocks, and a star above a house. Invite learners to move a paper token using the clues, then extend a previously unseen bend-reach symbol pattern with handmade cards. Show two lightweight paper stands with different support widths; ask which one was more stable in a safe tabletop test and what was observed. If a response needs reteaching, model with a different example rather than requiring a full restart. Invite children to select Thursday’s design or a new drawing for a portfolio and describe one improvement.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner identifies or uses three position clues, extends an AB sequence, and describes one observed difference between paper models. Support with two location cards or a preassembled model; extend by designing a new route. Allow speech, pointing, seated actions, or accessible communication. Portfolio participation is not a formal mastery receipt; use the existing separate weekly assessment for any formal decision.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.
