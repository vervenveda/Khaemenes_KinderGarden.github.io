Day 1 · Properties, Not Appearance

Monday · Shape Detectives

Objective: Identify common 2D and 3D shapes by properties, explain that turning or resizing a shape does not change its identity, and describe at least one edge/corner/curve/face feature.

Teacher model

Turn it—still the same shape

  1. Show a square upright and turned like a diamond. Count four equal sides/corners.
  2. Say: “Turning the square changes its position, not its properties.”
  3. Compare a flat circle picture with a ball/sphere: both look round in some views, but one is a 2D shape and one is a 3D solid.
  4. Invite touch/trace language: straight edge, curved edge, corner, flat face, rolls, stacks.
Movement geometry

Make shapes with your body

Use arms, hands, yarn, a partner, or floor space to make a line, curve, triangle-like outline, or rectangle-like outline. Rotate your body shape and ask what stayed the same.

Scaffold: count sides/corners together. Extension: find two different-looking examples of the same shape in the environment.

Day 2 · Spatial Language + Composition

Tuesday · Compose & Locate

Objective: Use positional words accurately and compose familiar shapes into a larger figure or design.

Teacher model

Words tell where

  1. Place a small object above, below, beside, inside, and between other objects.
  2. Ask the child to move an object from one position to another.
  3. Join two square paper pieces side by side and notice the larger rectangle-like outline.
  4. Combine triangles, rectangles, circles, or solids to make a recognizable design.
Language inclusion

Say it, show it, move it

Positional understanding may be demonstrated by moving an object correctly even when expressive language is still developing. Home-language spatial words are valid evidence when the relationship is understood.

Extension: give a two-step direction: “Put the circle above the square and the triangle beside the circle.”

Day 3 · Classify + Artistic Inquiry

Wednesday · Attribute & Color Lab

Objective: Sort the same objects by different attributes, explain the sorting rule, and explore color mixing or layering as an observation rather than a memorized rule.

Teacher model

One object can belong to many groups

  1. Sort a set by shape. State the rule: “I put all circles together.”
  2. Resort the same set by size, texture, use, or color.
  3. Ask: “Did the objects change, or did our rule change?”
  4. In art, layer or mix two available colors and simply observe the result. Different pencils, crayons, paints, screens, lights, papers, and pigments can behave differently.
Color-accessible design

Color is one attribute—not the only one

Use shape labels, patterns, textures, outlines, words, or tactile markers alongside color. No child should have to distinguish two colors to demonstrate sorting or geometry.

Universal art option: if mixing materials are unavailable, layer pencil marks, compare light/dark pressure, use black-and-white patterns, or describe color relationships from a safe image.

Day 4 · Engineer + Revise

Thursday · Shape City Engineers

Objective: Plan and create a Shape City using geometric forms and spatial relationships, test one design choice, and revise or explain why it works.

Design challenge

Build a city that can be described mathematically

  1. Plan at least three structures, routes, signs, or spaces.
  2. Use at least three different shapes or solids.
  3. Place features using words such as beside, between, above, below, inside, in front of, or behind.
  4. Test one design idea: Does a block stack? Does the road connect? Can someone follow the map?
  5. Revise something or explain why no change was needed.
Safe / accessible building

Any medium can become a city

Use paper shapes, recycled boxes, blocks, bottle caps, tactile pieces, a drawing, a floor arrangement, or spoken placement directions. Avoid glass, sharp objects, unstable heavy stacks, or choking hazards around younger children.

Persistence: a design change is evidence of thinking—not failure.

Day 5 · Retrieve + Explain Design

Friday · Design Tour

Objective: Retrieve core geometry ideas, explain a design using shape properties and positional language, and identify one successful revision or next step before formal mastery.

Gallery language

Describe instead of merely naming

Invite: “I used a ___ because…”, “The ___ is beside the ___,” “This shape has…”, “I changed ___ when…”. A child may present orally, sign, use AAC, dictate, point through a model, or use a home language.

Growth mindset

Designs improve through evidence

Celebrate a revision, not just a finished product. Ask what the learner noticed, what changed, and whether the change solved the design problem.

🗣 Five Geometry Retrievals

1. A square turned on a corner is…

2. Which describes a circle?

3. If a star is over a house, the star is…

4. Can the same objects be sorted by shape and then sorted again by size?

5. What can help improve a design?

Try all five. Four correct shows readiness for the design-tour reflection; retry is always allowed.
