Monday: Beat Detectives

Daily objective: Keep a steady four-beat pulse and distinguish it from a changing rhythm.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How can patterns become art and music?”

Literacy: Describe art and music

Math: Patterns, shapes, and counting beats

Inquiry: Sound, vibration, and design

SEL: Creative courage

Original guided activity: Use four original paper beat circles as a visual score. An adult points to each circle at an even pace while making four gentle taps; children echo with fingers, soft hand taps, a paper pointer, or a silent gesture. Keep the same four steady beats while trying a spoken pattern such as “ta, ta-ta, ta” over them, then ask what stayed steady and what changed. Repeat slowly, allowing children to point rather than make sound. Beat means a recurring pulse; rhythm describes the pattern of long and short sounds or silences. No screen, audio service, or instrument purchase is required.

Practice check (not formal mastery): Learner indicates four evenly spaced beats and distinguishes the steady pulse from a changed spoken rhythm by pointing, tapping, describing, or using accessible communication. Support with two beats before returning to four; extend by inventing a different rhythm while preserving the pulse. Retry with a new example rather than restarting the lesson.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Tuesday: Musical Pattern Makers

Daily objective: Create and explain a repeating AB or AAB pattern using colors and sounds.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How can patterns become art and music?”

Literacy: Describe art and music

Math: Patterns, shapes, and counting beats

Inquiry: Sound, vibration, and design

SEL: Creative courage

Original guided activity: Prepare six original paper sound cards: three circles labeled tap and three squares labeled hush, with colors as a secondary cue rather than the only distinction. Model AB as tap–hush, tap–hush and AAB as tap–tap–hush, tap–tap–hush. Children first arrange the cards, then point, tap gently, or perform a silent action for each symbol. Ask learners to mark where a repeating unit begins and ends, predict the next two symbols, and invent a new pattern using the same two symbols. For clarity, distinguish the pattern unit from the steady four-beat pulse practiced Monday; patterns need not be restricted to four beats. All cards and examples are original and can be made offline.

Practice check (not formal mastery): Learner independently extends a new AB or AAB sequence and identifies its repeating unit by words, pointing, drawing, or accessible communication. Support with an AB sequence of four cards and adult modeling; extend by translating a pattern from cards into a gentle sound sequence and back. If a prediction is incorrect, model one different example and offer another attempt without restarting the lesson.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Wednesday: Sound Scientists Lab

Daily objective: Observe how changing tempo differs from changing loudness.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How can patterns become art and music?”

Literacy: Describe art and music

Math: Patterns, shapes, and counting beats

Inquiry: Sound, vibration, and design

SEL: Creative courage

Original guided activity: Make two original picture cards marked slow and fast, and two marked quiet and louder. Start with four gentle taps at a quiet, even speed; then repeat the same four taps faster while keeping them equally quiet. Children identify that tempo changed while loudness stayed similar. Next keep the same slow tempo and compare very gentle versus moderately audible taps on a soft surface, without sudden or excessive noise. Invite children to predict and describe each change through words, cards, pointing, or a silent visual demonstration. Explain that tempo describes how quickly the beats occur, while loudness describes how strong or quiet a sound seems; neither is the same as pitch. Avoid forcing listening or touching vibrating objects, and offer hearing-friendly silent card sequences.

Practice check (not formal mastery): Learner correctly identifies whether tempo or loudness changed in two newly modeled examples, and indicates one observable difference. Support with two picture choices and adult modeling; extend by proposing how to make a rhythm faster without making it louder. Give a different example for reteaching instead of requiring a full lesson restart.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Thursday: Little Composers Studio

Daily objective: Compose and revise a four-beat musical phrase using a picture score.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How can patterns become art and music?”

Literacy: Describe art and music

Math: Patterns, shapes, and counting beats

Inquiry: Sound, vibration, and design

SEL: Creative courage

Original guided activity: Draw a four-box picture score using an original symbol key: circle = gentle tap, square = soft clap, star = quiet pause. Each box represents one beat; follow the boxes in order at an even, comfortable pulse. Invite the child to choose a four-symbol sequence, point to or perform it, and tell what each mark means. Change exactly one symbol, replay or point through the revised score, and compare the two versions. Let children name their piece and share privately or with a willing partner. Use safe paper materials, optional silent gestures, and no external music service or recording requirement. Explain that a pause still occupies a beat in this simple score.

Practice check (not formal mastery): Learner produces an understandable four-beat visual score, demonstrates or indicates its sequence, and identifies one deliberate change between versions. Support with a pre-drawn four-box score and two symbols; extend by explaining how a different order changes the phrase while the four-beat length stays the same. Respond to errors with modeling and a fresh attempt, not a lesson restart.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Friday: Little Composers Concert and Reflection

Daily objective: Identify a repeating unit in a new sound pattern and share a creative choice.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “How can patterns become art and music?”

Literacy: Describe art and music

Math: Patterns, shapes, and counting beats

Inquiry: Sound, vibration, and design

SEL: Creative courage

Original guided activity: Begin with a fresh set of educator-drawn sound cards distinct from Tuesday’s examples: triangle = gentle tap and diamond = silent gesture. Show triangle–diamond–diamond, triangle–diamond–diamond, and ask children to find the three-card repeating unit and predict the next three cards. Compare this A-B-B pattern with one child-created four-beat picture score from Thursday. Invite optional sharing in a small group, with an adult, or silently by pointing; never require public performance or recording. Ask each child to identify one musical choice they made and one change they might try next. Revisit misunderstandings with new cards rather than repeating a memorized example.

Practice check (not formal mastery): Learner extends an unfamiliar repeating sound-symbol pattern, identifies its repeating unit, and communicates a deliberate composition choice or revision. Support with a simpler AB card pattern and guided pointing; extend by composing a different AAB or ABB pattern and showing where it repeats. Participation and reflection are practice evidence only; use the existing separate weekly assessment for formal mastery decisions.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.
