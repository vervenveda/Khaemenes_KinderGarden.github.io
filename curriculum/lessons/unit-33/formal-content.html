Monday: Day and Night Sky Detectives

Daily learning target: Identify day and night in original sky illustrations, order a simple day-to-night sequence, and explain that Earth turning changes which part faces the Sun.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “What patterns can we see in the sky?”

Literacy: Sky words and question sentences

Math: Sequence day/night and count sky observations

Inquiry: Sun, moon, stars, and day/night patterns

SEL: Wonder and patience

Original guided activity: Draw three original paper scenes showing morning, afternoon, and nighttime from one location. Invite children to observe clues, sequence the cards, and name one change they can see. Use a large paper circle marked Earth with a dot for a pretend observer and a stationary yellow paper Sun: slowly rotate Earth so the dot faces toward and then away from the Sun. Explain that sunlight reaches the side of Earth facing the Sun; night occurs on the side turned away, not because the Sun switches off. Mention that the Moon can sometimes be visible during the day and stars are not only present at night. No direct Sun viewing, dark room, internet videos, or external astronomy tools are needed.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner sorts or sequences two or three sky scenes, identifies a day/night clue, and describes or points to the Earth model changing direction relative to the stationary Sun. Support with two picture choices and adult demonstration; extend by predicting what happens when Earth turns farther. Accept speech, drawing, pointing, or accessible communication and provide a fresh example for reteaching without a lesson restart.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Tuesday: Moon Shape Observation Lab

Daily learning target: Recognize and compare three illustrated Moon appearances, describe what is visibly illuminated, and explain that the Moon does not change its actual shape.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “What patterns can we see in the sky?”

Literacy: Sky words and question sentences

Math: Sequence day/night and count sky observations

Inquiry: Sun, moon, stars, and day/night patterns

SEL: Wonder and patience

Original guided activity: Draw three original Moon picture cards: a thin crescent, a half-lit Moon, and a full-looking Moon. Children place the pictures in order from least to most of the visible face illuminated, count the three examples, and describe their observations using crescent, half-lit, and full. Clarify that these are selected examples, not every phase or a complete month-long sequence. Model the Moon as one unchanged paper circle with different parts of its sunlit half visible from Earth as the Moon orbits us; the usual monthly phases are not caused by Earth’s shadow. Invite children to draw one appearance and dictate a question they could investigate through future careful observations. No nighttime visit, telescope, bright light, or direct Sun viewing is required.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner matches or orders the three original Moon cards by visible illumination, correctly states or indicates that the Moon stays the same shape, and describes one visible difference. Support with two cards and educator modeling; extend by comparing a waxing and waning crescent using additional original drawings without claiming every observation follows a simple three-step sequence. Accept speech, drawing, pointing, or accessible communication. Offer a new example rather than resetting completed work when reteaching.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Wednesday: Sun, Earth, and Moon Model Investigators

Daily learning target: Identify the Sun as our source of daylight, model Earth turning to produce day and night, and describe how the Moon travels around Earth without confusing these movements with Moon phases.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “What patterns can we see in the sky?”

Literacy: Sky words and question sentences

Math: Sequence day/night and count sky observations

Inquiry: Sun, moon, stars, and day/night patterns

SEL: Wonder and patience

Original guided activity: Prepare three original paper circles labeled Sun, Earth, and Moon, with different sizes marked “not to scale.” Keep the Sun card in one position. Put a small paper house mark on Earth and rotate the Earth card in place: when the mark faces the Sun it is day there, and when it faces away it is night. Next move the Moon card around Earth while leaving Earth in place for this second demonstration. Ask which motion represents Earth turning and which represents the Moon orbiting Earth. Explain that the Moon also turns as it travels and the changing portion of its sunlit half that we see creates its phases; everyday Moon phases are not Earth’s shadow. Model one motion at a time to avoid implying that this flat paper model shows exact distances, sizes, or all three-dimensional lighting. No actual lights, direct Sun viewing, or third-party materials are required.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner identifies the three paper objects, shows or points to Earth turning for day and night, and distinguishes this from the Moon moving around Earth. Support with two motion cards and guided modeling; extend by predicting day or night for a second mark on the opposite side of Earth. Accept speech, drawing, pointing, or accessible communication. Provide a new example for reteaching without restarting prior progress.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “What patterns can we see in the sky?”

Literacy: Sky words and question sentences

Math: Sequence day/night and count sky observations

Inquiry: Sun, moon, stars, and day/night patterns

SEL: Wonder and patience

Workshop: Create a two-part sky journal page: draw or arrange one day/night scene and one Moon appearance, add labels or dictated notes, and connect each claim to a visible clue. Offer picture cards, AAC, tactile symbols, or verbal explanation as equivalent options.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “What patterns can we see in the sky?”
