Monday: Day and Night Sky Detectives

Daily learning target: Identify day and night in original sky illustrations, order a simple day-to-night sequence, and explain that Earth turning changes which part faces the Sun.

Morning Circle: Welcome song, visual schedule, feelings check-in, and question of the day: “What patterns can we see in the sky?”

Literacy: Sky words and question sentences

Math: Sequence day/night and count sky observations

Inquiry: Sun, moon, stars, and day/night patterns

SEL: Wonder and patience

Original guided activity: Draw three original paper scenes showing morning, afternoon, and nighttime from one location. Invite children to observe clues, sequence the cards, and name one change they can see. Use a large paper circle marked Earth with a dot for a pretend observer and a stationary yellow paper Sun: slowly rotate Earth so the dot faces toward and then away from the Sun. Explain that sunlight reaches the side of Earth facing the Sun; night occurs on the side turned away, not because the Sun switches off. Mention that the Moon can sometimes be visible during the day and stars are not only present at night. No direct Sun viewing, dark room, internet videos, or external astronomy tools are needed.

Practice evidence (not formal mastery): Learner sorts or sequences two or three sky scenes, identifies a day/night clue, and describes or points to the Earth model changing direction relative to the stationary Sun. Support with two picture choices and adult demonstration; extend by predicting what happens when Earth turns farther. Accept speech, drawing, pointing, or accessible communication and provide a fresh example for reteaching without a lesson restart.

Centers: Teacher table, Math/manipulative bin, Art/maker station, Dramatic play, Movement/music, Book corner, Calm reflection spot

Teacher evidence: Adult observation, child explanation, work sample, oral language, cooperative play, and assessment item.
